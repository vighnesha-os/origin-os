## Parser hardening (CLAUDE.md §13, §72)

Every parser in the host-tested library that reads firmware or device data has a deterministic, seeded mutation soak on `crate::testing`: the ACPI tables (RSDP, XSDT, FADT, MADT, SRAT, SLIT, MCFG, HPET), PCI configuration space and capabilities, NVMe registers and Identify, GPT, the CMOS RTC, virtio capabilities and rings, CPUID decoders, the boot info, the archive and filesystem formats and the network parsers. Each soak checks that nothing panics (overflow checks on), nothing reads out of bounds, and anything accepted keeps its documented invariants, round-tripping where an encoder exists. Checksummed structures are re-sealed so the checks behind them are reached. A default run is about 1.3 million mutated inputs and takes 1 s. The soaks found 13 bugs in the library (a CPUID overflow, an on-disk arithmetic overflow, device-reported virtqueue ids that panicked, virtio capabilities read past the capability area and taken in the wrong order, and others) and kernel paths that trusted what the parsers returned (structures and MSI-X tables outside their memory BARs, a queue size that was not a power of two, doorbells past BAR 0). Each is fixed with a regression test, and each test is shown to fail with its fix reverted. The malformed-device paths in the kernel binary can only be shown on a fault-injecting device model or hardware (§74). The full inventory and per-parser results are in `docs/engineering/SOURCE_PROVENANCE.md`.

The ONBF parsers, which run in the boot loader on data read before any signature is checked, have their own seeded multi-fault soaks: one to four faults per case over the container, OCBOR-1, the KRN manifest and the signature block, plus Merkle and signature verification, each judged by a model written from the specification (re-encoding byte for byte where an encoder exists, and compared in both directions). A default run is about 98,000 cases and adds about 1 s to `cargo xtask check`. They found five conformance bugs, none a panic or an out-of-bounds read, since all of these crates are panic-free by lint and by the object check: a mapped MERKLE or DWARF section was accepted, a certificate chain longer than four, a statement version that is not SemVer, and a block larger than 1 MiB passed straight to `SignatureBlock::parse`. Each is fixed with a regression test and shown to fail with its fix reverted.

## Concurrency models (CLAUDE.md §73; 2026-11-08)

`cargo xtask loom`, also part of `cargo xtask check`, model-checks the real `SpinLock`, the real `RwLock`, the real wait-queue protocol and the real run queue under Loom 0.7.2, from a scratch workspace with its own committed lock file: the root `Cargo.lock` holds only workspace packages, so building the kernel needs no network, and `cargo xtask check` fails if a third-party package appears there. To run under Loom the protocol moved out of the kernel binary, unchanged, into `origin_sync::wait`.

**Proved, within each model's printed bounds** (26 models, each with negative controls: 35 in all; every interleaving up to Loom's reduction, in Loom's model of C11): one `SpinLock` holder at a time, no data race on the protected value, and each holder sees earlier holders' writes whole; `try_lock` likewise; an `RwLock` writer overlaps nobody, readers can hold it together and see its updates whole; and **no lost wake-up**: a waiter whose condition is made true by a thread that then calls `wake_one` does not stay blocked, even with `Relaxed` on the condition (the queue's lock carries the ordering), a wait returns only with its condition true, a timed-out waiter leaves no registration behind, threads that cannot register still see the condition by polling, and a timed wait on the boot flow polls and ends with the condition or the deadline. 22 negative controls (weakened orderings, a removed wake-up, an early wake-up forgotten by the run queue, a condition read outside the lock, and others), each applied to a scratch copy of the source, make the models fail with the expected report; every model has one.

**Not proved:** anything past the bounds; what Loom's memory model omits (load buffering, spurious `compare_exchange_weak` failures); interrupts, the context switch, IPIs and timers, which the models stand in for; fairness and starvation; the x86-64 machine code; a timed waiter that finds the queue full. The full statement, the bounds and the 30 approved third-party crates with their licences and sizes are in `docs/engineering/SOURCE_PROVENANCE.md`, "Loom models".

## Boot proofs under host load (CLAUDE.md §68, §72)

Every boot proof that times one clock against another now tolerates a loaded host. Under a loaded host a virtual CPU is descheduled for milliseconds, and each such proof read that as the clock being wrong:

- the RTC second: updates are seen through the seconds register, which cannot be missed, instead of the 244 µs update-in-progress pulse QEMU shows (a pulse was missed at 29 % of updates under load), and each is located between the polls around it (`rtc::UpdateWatcher`). A measurement decides only if its whole interval of uncertainty is inside or outside 1 % (`rtc::SecondMeter`), so a stall costs a measurement, never a wrong verdict; a vague pair gives way to the next edge, at most 3.2 s in all.
- the PIT calibration of the TSC: a 10 ms window with a stall at either end (or between programming the count and raising the gate) is recognised and measured again (`apic::CalibrationWindow`); before, such stalls corrupted the TSC rate by up to 39 %, which was behind all but one of the `one RTC second lasted … clock failed` boots.
- the cross-CPU TSC check keeps going, to 500 ms at most, until the processors have really interleaved; the HPET and PM-timer reads are paired tightly with the TSC (`clock::closest_read`); and `prove_timers` repeats a run in which the polling loop itself stalled.

With every host core loaded (10 busy-loop processes, 30 boots each, alternating boot by boot), `cargo xtask boot-test`'s failure rate went from 83 % (the original kernel) to 3 % (this one: 1 boot in 30, the scheduler's weighted-share bound, untouched here). RTC-wait timeouts went from 11 of 30 boots to 0. Twelve negative controls fail the boot: frozen and stuck RTCs, an RTC 3 % slow, an RTC at 2× and at ½ speed, a PIT rate 3 % high, a corrupted PIT window accepted, an AP's TSC skewed by 5000 ticks, handoffs unreachable, the HPET and PM timer 3 % fast, and a timer genuinely late while the poller runs. Three positive controls show a stall is forgiven, not hidden: an injected stall in a PIT window, in an HPET read and in a timer run is each detected and retried, and the boot passes. Evidence: `docs/engineering/SOURCE_PROVENANCE.md`, "The clock proofs under host load".

The scheduler's weighted-share proof (weights 1, 4 and 16 on one processor for 2 s), the last boot proof still failing at one `yes` per logical core, has the same treatment. It counted loop iterations, and on a loaded host the iterations in a 10 ms slice vary by more than tenfold whatever the scheduler did, while the lightest thread's share is the sum of about ten slices; so each worker now reports the time it saw itself running (steps under 3 ms), with the bound unchanged and pinned by a host test. A run whose shares are wrong although its processor visibly lost timer ticks or was away for over a tenth of the window is measured again, at most three runs in all; a wrong result from an undisturbed run fails at once. Over 150 runs of the proof per measure with every host core loaded, the old iteration count broke the bound once and the new measure never; at double load (two `yes` per core) 9 times (6 %) and twice (1.3 %, both visibly disturbed, so repeated). In `cargo xtask boot-test`, alternating the two kernels boot by boot, neither failed in 30 boots at one `yes` per core; at two per core the old kernel failed this proof in 2 boots of 15 and this change in none (`timers`, a different proof, failed one boot of each). Negative controls fail the boot (a wrong weight, weights ignored, a worker never picked, a wrong pick, no preemption, and a step limit so large that preemptions count), and a 70 ms stall injected into a run is repeated and passes, and fails if the repeat is disabled. Evidence: `docs/engineering/SOURCE_PROVENANCE.md`, "The weighted-share proof under host load".

The two proofs still failing at double load (two `yes` per logical core) have had the same treatment. `prove_timers` now brackets every firing between the polls around it, and a run decides only if each firing's whole interval is inside or outside the 2 ms limit (`timers::judge`): a firing the queue held past the limit, or a wrong order, fails at once, and a run a stall left undecided is measured again, up to 64 runs (six before; a run is about 15 ms and an undisturbed boot still pays for one). In 36,000 firings at up to four `yes` per core none was decisively late, so every lateness the old proof reported was a late look; at double load the six-run budget ran out for 4.4 % of the starting points in 3600 runs, and 24 runs never ran out. The ping-pong between two processors (1000 round trips) still passes only if a run's mean round trip is under 2000 µs, the old bound. A run over it is measured again if the boot flow's own loop went without a turn for at least a tenth of the run, at most 8 runs in all; without that reading it fails at once. The reading only ever earns a repeat, because a thread on that processor can produce it just by running. The first version, `05deb69`, used it to swap the mean for the median and the share of reschedule IPIs; a review showed a kernel that delays itself passing that rule, and "the bound is unchanged" was not true of such a run, so it was replaced. A version judged on lost timer ticks on both processors was built and rejected too: a kernel holding interrupts off on both processors passes it. The mean is the host's tail on such a run: at double load the slowest tenth of the round trips carries 60 % of the time.

In `cargo xtask boot-test`, alternating the final rule against `118834b` boot by boot, no boot of either kernel failed the ping-pong at 2× (means of 1287 to 1722 µs) and the old kernel failed the timer proof in 1 of the 14 boots that reached it (`worst lateness 3111 us`) where the final rule passed all 14 (7 after a stalled run). Neither failed either proof at 1×. At 4× the host's ordinary round trip in this batch is over the bound: the final rule passed 2 of the 6 boots that reached the ping-pong, after repeats, and failed 4 after all 8 runs; the old kernel failed all 5 it reached. There the proof is measuring the host and says so by failing; most boots at 4× stopped earlier anyway, in the APIC timer and clock proofs, not touched here. The same kernel took about 2.5 times as long per round trip in this batch as in the day's earlier ones, so a count of `yes` does not by itself fix the stress. Also seen and not touched: the RTC proof once at 1×, the timed wait (81 ms, bound 80) once at 2×, and the iteration-counted two-worker fairness check in 2 boots of the final kernel at 2× and 4×, 2 of 105 against 0 of 105 for the old kernel, which is too few to tell from chance. Negative controls fail the boot: a timer 3 ms late with the poller running, a cancelled timer firing, a queue that never fires; for the ping-pong, the pinger or the ponger delaying itself 10 ms on every fourth round trip, holds with interrupts off on either or both sides, no reschedule IPI, every second or fourth IPI lost, a 3 ms slow wake path, and on one processor every fourth round trip 10 ms slow. Positive controls: a 9 ms stall across a timer deadline, and a 2.5 s stall of one or both processors in the first run, are repeated and pass, and fail with the repeat disabled. Everything is QEMU TCG. Evidence: `docs/engineering/SOURCE_PROVENANCE.md`, "The wait-queue ping-pong and the timer queue under host load".

On the merged IOMMU branch (`31b4347`), `cargo xtask boot-matrix` passed in full on its first attempt with the host at load 50–260 (mean 168) and another session's QEMU running for most of it: 8 kernel boots, one `timers` run repeated after a stall, and 41 calibration windows discarded over 6 of those boots. Five attempts on the tree before this merge failed at load 57–170 or with another session's boots running. That is one run, not a failure rate.

With the early console and the wait-queue and timer fixes merged (`31c3d39`), `cargo xtask check` passes (827 tests, 0 failures, 6 ignored) and `cargo xtask boot-matrix` passes on a quiet host (load 2.7–4.0), on its first attempt: 8 kernel boots and the 8 console boots. All 15 ping-pongs passed at their first run (41 to 56 µs on two or more processors, 5 µs on one), and all 15 `prove_timers` runs decided at their first run (worst lateness 0 µs in 14, 38 µs in one). Drawing a console line took 226 to 245 µs on average and at most 808 µs, under QEMU TCG. That is one run on a quiet host and says nothing about double load, where the measurements above stand: at four busy-loop processes per core the ping-pong still fails most of the boots that reach it.
