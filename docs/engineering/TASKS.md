Each read sits between two TSC reads and is taken again when they are far apart, as the calibration discards a disturbed window. The count runs down whether or not an interrupt is delivered, so a starved processor does not move it; a wrong divisor, or an APIC rate that disagrees with the TSC's, does. Proposed bound: the calibration's own spread, which the design measures across the matrix, and never wider than ±5 %, the tolerance the fast side has today. Today the slow side allows +50 %.
      - *Delivery, on its own:* that the timer's interrupts arrive on their vector, period after period. On a processor that reports no hypervisor (CPUID.01H:ECX[31], which `speculation.rs` already reads), every period is delivered: 50 interrupts within the rate's bound of 500 ms. Under a hypervisor, merged periods are allowed but silence is not. Proposed bound: all 50 within the two seconds the proof already waits, four times the nominal time. The slowest kept log took 2.6 times, so the design narrows that bound if its starved runs show it can be.
    - **CI's real-processor boots run under a hypervisor too:** KVM (`ci.yml`'s `boot-real-cpu`, and `processor-sweep.yml`). So the hypervisor's rule applies there as on the Mac, and what is still checked there is the rate from the count against the TSC, at the same narrow bound as everywhere, and delivery at the hypervisor's bound. The design checks that those processors report the hypervisor bit. No machine without a hypervisor has run this proof (CLAUDE.md §74), so the bare-metal bound is untested until one does.
    - **A starved run without loading the machine** for other sessions: stop and continue the QEMU process (SIGSTOP, SIGCONT) at a known duty cycle while the proof runs. Such a run should show merged periods, a rate that holds, and where the delivery bound can sit.
    - **Negative controls:** a wrong divisor; an APIC rate off by more than the bound, in each direction; the timer's interrupt masked; one-shot mode in place of periodic. Each must fail under TCG and under KVM.
    - **ADR-0013.** Its row for the APIC timer says a correct kernel fails this proof only when no calibration source works (since F3). The kept logs show it failing with one that works, under a starved emulator. R1(a) already gives the remedy: a better proof, not a looser rule. A change to that row, or to what the proof refuses, is the owner's (R7: the table is the policy).

**What the contract settles (the review note in item 18).** None of S1 to S7 changes R4, R6 or R7 as accepted: each fills something those rules leave open, and each says which words it rests on. If building shows that one cannot be kept without changing them, the work stops there and the change goes to the owner as an amendment first (§84).

- **S1. Where draws run, and how long interrupts stay off.** The generator's work is done in steps, each with interrupts off, and interrupts are restored between steps to what the caller had. There are three kinds of step: one try of one instruction, with the continuous tests on its value and the value put into the processor's own store; one piece of a request, at most 128 bytes (R7); one reseed or instantiation from a full store. Counting tries, pausing between them and deciding the next step happen between steps and touch nothing secret. So the longest time the generator keeps interrupts off is one step, whatever the retry budget is. R6's "before a request" and R7's "a request runs with interrupts off" both hold: the reseed's steps come before the request's first piece.
- **S2. How much one request may do.** At most 8 draws for a reseed that is due, and at most 8 more towards a start-up test that is being run again: 16 draws, each with R4's budget of tries. A start-up test that has to be run again (R4 item 8) starts when the processor's generator next falls due for a reseed and goes on over the following requests, 8 draws at a time, until 1,024 consecutive draws have passed or one fails. R4 says the test "is tried" then; it does not say one request carries all of it.
- **S3. A dry RDSEED: what backs off, and when RDRAND takes over.** One draw whose 100 tries were all "not ready" is R4's "not now". When a reseed that is due meets it, RDSEED on that processor is left alone for one second, and the reseed is made at once from 8 RDRAND draws where RDRAND is healthy; the RDSEED draws already gathered go in as additional input, so no draw is used twice or thrown away. It counts as a reseed, and a counter the boot report shows says how many were made that way. With no healthy RDRAND, or RDRAND "not now" too, the reseed stays due and nothing draws again until the second has passed. So a program that keeps RDSEED dry (T5) costs one draw's tries a second on each processor, not on each request. A request for prediction resistance is refused with "not now" where its 8 RDSEED draws cannot be had (R6). These are T5's words, "delayed" and "fall back to RDRAND", given numbers.
- **S4. The root's lock.** The processor that needs the root gathers its 8 draws into its own store first, as in S1. It then takes the root's lock for one step: the reseed and the generation. No instruction is tried while the lock is held, and a processor that finds the lock taken waits with its interrupts restored.
- **S5. The stack overwrite covers the draws.** Every step of S1 runs on a stack that belongs to the generator, one for each processor, 8 KiB with a guard page below it. Before interrupts come back on, the whole of that stack is overwritten and the scratch registers are cleared. A draw therefore never rests on a thread's stack, and the overwrite does not depend on an estimate of depth: this is R9 Z3 with "a fixed depth" made the whole stack. The store a draw goes into and the tests' few remembered values (the last draw, a window's first draw, two masks) are in the processor's static storage with its state.
- **S6. A caller that arrives with interrupts off** (an interrupt handler, R7) gets R6's reseed before its request like any other, with one try for each draw in place of R4's "up to" 100 and 10, so that it never waits in a pause. That caller's own time with interrupts off then grows by at most 16 tries and one reseed.
- **S7. What a non-maskable interrupt can leave.** An NMI or a machine check that arrives in the middle of a step saves the interrupted registers on its own stack, which is not overwritten. Neither handler asks for random values (R7). This is named, not closed: it is a limit of R9 that T11 does not state, and it goes into ADR-0016's "cannot show" list with this task.
