## 2. Findings

### R130-1 — High — `latitude.py:1115-1116`, `1227-1235`: a `Y` after `X` is read as the firmware's restart, and a retry is never shown

**What is wrong.**

- `MARK_AT` consumes only one mark. When the loader writes `X\r\n` and then `Y\r\n` (the designed retry after a failed first call), `firmware_after(raw, end)` returns the `Y`. The call is then read as `reset`, with `after_s` equal to the gap between X and Y.
- `MARKS` is matched with non-overlapping `finditer`. Its match of `\nX\r\n` uses up the `\n` that precedes `Y`, so `marks` is never `XY`. A failed first call is invisible even in the two-failure path.

**Scenario.** Probes run on the code as committed:

- **X, then Y 1 s later, then the retry hangs.** Read as "a call (PMxC0 0x00900800), X, then the firmware again 1 s after its mark". In the live loop, `boot_over` ends the boot `load + SLACK_S` after the `Y`, so the record claims a reset that never happened.
- **X, then Y, then the retry succeeds** (a triple fault, and the firmware 48.8 s later). Read as "reset 1 s after its mark", with marks `X`.
- **X, then Y, then the halting line, then Amendment 3's reset.** The answer is right (48.5 s after the line), but marks are `X` and the text says "after its mark".

**Fix.**

- Make `MARK_AT` consume every consecutive mark: `rb"(?:[ \t]*[XY]\r?\n)+"`.
- Collect marks with `rb"(?m)^([XY])\r?$"`, or with a lookbehind `(?<=\n)`.
- Measure from the last mark.
- Add tests for X+Y+reset, X+Y+hang, and X+Y+halting line.

### R130-2 — Medium — `latitude.py:1166-1180`, `1233`, `1259-1271`: any byte after the last one is "the firmware again"

**What is wrong.** Anything that is not white space and not on the allow-list of three client messages counts as a firmware start. Nothing checks that it looks like one, in the console's shape or in its timing. The docstring's premise covers the loader but not the firmware: firmware can print without restarting. `own_reset` also wins over the hang rule, so a reset seen after the hang threshold still reads as a reset.

**Scenarios.**

- **X, then a firmware exception dump 0.5 s later, then a hang.** Read as "the firmware again 0.5 s after its mark".
- **An unlisted SOL client line during a hang, before any restart.** Rental E shows the client printing during a hang at +76.5 s with no restart. A message outside `CLIENT`, such as one saying the BMC closed the session, would read as a reset.
- **A reset 300 s after the mark** (a BIOS or BMC watchdog, an 8-minute boot, or `reread` of rentals E and F). Read as "reset 300 s after its mark", although by the tool's own rule the call had hung at 93.5 s.

**Fix.**

- Call an answer `reset` only if the next byte is followed by a silence-then-start pattern. Or, more simply, only if `after_s` falls between a plausible floor (for example half the measured control delay, once one is known) and `hang`.
- Otherwise use a separate answer, such as "other output" or "a reset after the hang", that quotes the first bytes.

### R130-3 — Medium — `latitude.py:1235`: the branch "call" needs an `X`/`Y` mark

**What is wrong.** A run that printed the call line `ExitBootServices next ...`, but whose mark is missing, is "a run with neither control nor call". The mark can be missing in several ways:

- the SPCR names no UART;
- the SOL carries another port;
- the mark arrives inside the console's line, or after an escape sequence.

In each case `boot_over` never finds a call, and the boot runs to its window.

**Scenarios.**

- Call line with no `X`, then a reset → "a run with neither control nor call (PMxC0 0x00900800), then the firmware again 48.8 s after its line".
- `X` interleaved in the line → "other, the firmware again 0 s after its line".

**Fix.** Take the branch from `MINIMAL_LAST_LINE` (or "exiting boot services") and keep marks as an attribute. Say "a call, no mark seen" when there is no mark.

### R130-4 — Medium — `latitude.py:1611`, `1618`, `1672`, `1742-1746`: restarts reach the record only through completed boots

**What is wrong.**

- `runs_row` and `reread` take the restart times from `results` and `boots.json` (`restarted_at`), which are written only once a boot completes.
- A boot interrupted after its restart drops that restart from the record's "Runs" row and from `boots.json`. The interruption can be SIGTERM or SIGHUP (through `stop_on_signal`, a designed path), Ctrl-C, or an exception in `look()`.
- The run that the restart overtook then reads as `reset`.
- The time is also appended only after the API call returns, so a POST that raised after the BMC acted is never recorded.

**Scenario.** Rental G with R2 left out: run 3 reads "a call ..., X, then the firmware again 61.5 s after its mark". Those bytes were the restart's.

**Fix.**

- Append each restart to a file in the rental's directory before issuing it, and update it after.
- `runs_row` should use the loop's `restarts` list, or that file. `reread` should read the file.
- A restart that was attempted but not confirmed counts as a restart at the attempt time.

### R130-5 — Medium — `latitude.py:1431-1463`, `1626-1633`: `after_last_line` still counts the SOL client's lines and the tool's restarts

**What is wrong.** This commit changed `after_last_line`, but it still takes the next raw byte after the first call in the boot's range, and it still feeds `next_byte_s` and `next_loader_s` into every summary.

**Scenarios.**

- **Rental E, boot 3.** It now reads "the next byte came after 76.5 s". That byte is "Error: No response to keepalive" at 14:21:04.6, during a hang. The same summary's runs reading says "nothing for 365.9 s: no reset".
- **Rental G's range of boot 2.** It gives "61.5 s, next loader 113.9 s" after run 3's mark. Those are R2's bytes.

**Fix.** Use `firmware_after` and the restarts in `after_last_line`, or drop those two figures for any image read by `read_runs`.

### R130-6 — Medium — `test_latitude.py:531-664`: the tests pass real bugs

I planted faults in a scratch copy, with the history check pointed at the worktree so that all 45 tests run. Each fault below passes all 45 tests:

- **M1:** the `restarted` path of `figures()["restart"]` deleted, so the restart delay is never measured from `restart_start`. The replay and `test_a_restarts_start_is_after_the_longest_silence` both expect 68.0, which equals `PLAN_RESTART_S`.
- **M2:** `Y` dropped from `MARKS`. No test has a `Y`.
- **M3:** `own_reset` accepts bytes up to 30 s after a restart was issued. Every in-flight test puts the next byte 60 s or more after the restart.
- **M4:** the hang threshold's default changed from 49 s to 0 when no control has been measured.

Also untested:

- a call with no mark (R130-3);
- an interrupted boot (R130-4);

The replay pins rental G well, but every call in G has the same shape: X, then the firmware 48.8 s later. So the replay cannot catch bugs in the branch or the marks.

- Assert `start` and `restarted` for runs 8, 10 and 12 in the replay.

- Add the Y, no-mark, after-text and interrupted-boot cases.

### R130-7 — Low — `latitude.py:1272-1279`, `1316-1317`: "none" overstates the silence that was checked

**What is wrong.** A reset in the last `start` seconds before the tool's restart, or before `until`, produces its first byte after that time, so it is read as "none". "Then nothing for 93.5 s: no reset" establishes no reset only for `waited − start`, about 45 s after the mark.

**Fix.** Word it as "no reset within {waited_s − start} s of the mark".

### R130-8 — Low — `latitude.py:1237`, `1268-1279`: bytes logged but not yet sampled read as "none"

**What is wrong.** A byte in `sol.log` that is not yet in `sol-times.log` (up to 0.5 s) has an arrival of `None`. `read_runs` then treats it as absent.

**Scenario.** The probe gives "none, nothing for 93.6 s" while firmware bytes sit in the log. In the live loop the boot ends on this transient, and the boot's summary then disagrees with the record's re-read.

**Fix.** If `nxt` is not `None` and its arrival is `None`, answer `pending`.

### R130-9 — Low — `latitude.py:1183-1197`: a client message split across two samples counts as firmware

**What is wrong.** `restart_start` scans sample by sample. `CLIENT.match` only matches from a message's first byte, so when a message is split across two samples, its tail in the second sample is counted as firmware. That puts a false "seen" time inside the restart's silence.

**Fix.** Scan the range once for firmware byte offsets, then map those offsets to times.

### R130-10 — Low — `read_runs` (whole function): console gaps are not consulted

**What is wrong.** `sol-events.log` records the session's drops and reopenings, but the reading never consults it. A gap longer than the firmware's output after a reset (about 50 s on rental G, 40 s on rental F) can hide a reset. Such gaps are possible through the five-quick-failures reopen through the API. A hidden reset is read as "none", or as a late "reset".

**Fix.** Read the session gaps, and answer "undetermined" when a gap overlaps `[end, end + hang]`.

### R130-11 — Low — `latitude.py:1095`, `1285`, `1280-1281`, `1310`; `test_latitude.py` (window test): text and comments

- **Line 1095** says 6.6 to 7.1 s from a reset to the firmware's first byte on rental F. The tool itself measures 3.5 s there: the 14-byte clear screen at 04:10:55.8 and 04:18:56.7. The 6.6 to 7.1 s is the time to the "Pre-memory" line. The window test uses 7.1 for "rental F's 2.5".
- **Line 1285:** the `restarts` parameter of `figures` is unused.
- **`runs_summary`:** "a reset after N of the M the tool's restarts did not overtake" leaves out pending calls. On rental F this prints "0 of the 2", although 3 calls were not overtaken.
- **`describe`:** says "after its mark" when the end is a later loader line (the two-failure path, or the full bench mode's lines after the call).

### R130-12 — Low — `latitude.py:1152`, `1525-1534`: the plan's bound is not a bound

**What is wrong.**

- `round(window_s / 60, 1)` can understate the window by up to 3 s per boot.
- Each boot's actual window comes from the measured figures and can exceed the planned 5.8 minutes. So "39.8 minutes at most with the setup" is not a guarantee: later boots are then cut by the loop's stop, not by the plan.
- Money is unaffected (§3).

**Fix.** Round up (`math.ceil(x * 10) / 10`), and print the plan's figure as an estimate, or cap `window()` at what is left.

### R130-13 — Low — `latitude.py:1224-1237`, `1621-1625`: a partial last run is frozen into a boot's summary

**What is wrong.** When a boot ends at its window, its summary is read once. If that read lands in the middle of a line, the transient reading is what gets recorded.

**Scenarios.**

- A read in the middle of `origin-boot:` → "a run with neither control nor call, then the firmware again 0 s after its line".
- A read between `X` and its CR LF → the same.

The record's later re-read may differ.

**Fix.** Treat a run whose bytes end without a newline, or with a prefix of `origin-boot:`, as `pending`.
