## Findings

### T84-1 (Medium): the take-over can hand the console to a UART that was just given up on, leaving no UART at all

- **Where:** `framekernel/src/console.rs:1055-1097` (`take_over_loader_uart`): the liveness test at 1060 (`loader_port_on()`), then 1074-1078 (`drain_uart`, `sinks.loader.take()`, `sinks.uart_alive = line.alive`).
- **What is wrong:** the take-over tests once, before it writes its own line, whether the loader's UART is live. Two later steps both pump the loader's queue: that line's send (`send_and_draw`), and `drain_uart` under the lock. Either can end in `Pumped::Stopped`, which runs `give_up_on_loader_uart` (alive set false, the queue cleared, `LOADER_UART_ON` cleared). The closure then takes the dead `LoaderLine` anyway:
  - COM1's `Uart16550` and queue are dropped;
  - `uart_alive` becomes `false`;
  - `MOVED_UART` names the dead UART, so even lock-free reports go there.
- **Failing case:** a UART takes the first 2,800 or so bytes, then stops while the take-over line goes out. A BMC's virtual UART that stalls would do it, or a cable or flow-control fault.
  - COM1 shows `the loader's UART stopped taking bytes; serial output there off`, then `…; COM1 stops`.
  - The rest of the boot goes to the screen only: every proof, the verdict and every panic report.
  - On a server, where nobody captures the screen, the boot goes silent although COM1 works. That is the outcome Amendment 3 exists to prevent.
- **Fix:**
  - Inside the closure, after `drain_uart`, take the loader's line only if it is still alive. Otherwise keep COM1 (`sinks.loader = None`) and have the caller print `the loader's UART stopped during the move; the UART stays at I/O port 0x3f8`.
  - Better still, decide before announcing: queue the take-over line in the same critical section that makes the decision, so it never announces a take-over that did not happen.
  - Add a host test with two mock UARTs, where the second stops during the drain.

### T84-2 (Medium): while the loader's UART is live, one stretch with interrupts off covers two UART sections, and nothing measures or records that

- **Where:**
  - `console.rs:454-472`: `pump_uart` runs `pump_section` on each UART in one call, so in one critical section of `with_sinks` or `urgent_section`.
  - `console.rs:530-541`: `pump_section` times each half on its own.
  - The module documentation, `console.rs:23-34`: "a section is at most 17 register accesses".
  - ADR-0010 Amendment 2: "The bound is 1 status read and 16 data writes, 17 register accesses".
  - `PERFORMANCE_BUDGETS.md`: :38 (B18), :116 (CL15), :147 (CL46).
- **What is wrong:**
  - Each `pump_uart` holds the console lock, with interrupts off, across a section of COM1's queue and a section of the loader's queue: up to 2 status reads and 32 data writes, 34 register accesses.
  - The code's comment says "under the same bound and counted on its own", and each half is bounded. But the stretch with interrupts off is what Amendment 2 bounds and B18 budgets, and that stretch doubles.
  - `UART_SECTION_WORST_CYCLES` times each half, so two figures understate the stretch by up to half: the "us at worst" that `prove_uart_sections` prints, and B18's trend.
  - The module documentation does not mention the second UART.
- **Failing case:**
  - After a refused move (ruling Q1, rental B's case), both UARTs stay for the whole boot.
  - On a physical 16550 behind LPC or eSPI, each port access costs about a microsecond or more, so a stretch of 34 accesses is roughly 35 to 70 µs, against B18's 50 µs.
  - The worst section printed shows only one half.
  - Before the move the doubling does not matter: interrupts are off and no timer runs. After a take-over or a move, only one UART is left.
- **Fix:**
  - Pump one UART per critical section. Alternate between them, or let callers take a second section: they already loop until `reached`. Otherwise time the whole of `pump_uart` for B18.
  - Record the doubled stretch, or its removal, in CL15 and CL46 and in Amendment 3's text.
  - Update the module documentation.

### T84-3 (Medium): the only machine with two live UARTs through the console proofs holds none of them, and the proofs cannot tell the two UARTs apart

- **Where:**
  - xtask `main.rs`:
    - 2768-2779: `refused_scenario`;
    - 2785-2806: `REFUSED_FAILURES`;
    - 2816-2831: `check_refused_boot`;
    - 7248-7266: `the_refused_machine_holds_the_console`, which asserts that no failure marker ends in `" failed"`.
  - `console.rs`: 1620 and 1643-1645 (`prove_room`); 1675 and 1689-1692 (`prove_uart_sections`).
- **What is wrong: the refused machine checks no console proof.** After a refused move the console writes to COM1 and to the loader's UART for the rest of the boot. That happens through the console proofs only on this machine (`console::prove`, `prove_room`, `prove_uart_sections`, `prove_sections`).
  - Its failure list drops `: console failed`.
  - Its check runs none of the integrity checks the rented machine runs on COM2, on either UART: `console_lines_intact`, `long_lines_whole` and `unwaited_lines_whole`.
  - Its verdict marker is the bare prefix `origin-kernel: boot verdict: `, so a failing console proof leaves the scenario passing.
  - The record says "failed: …" and does not name the four proofs that failed. Whether the console proofs even ran with two UARTs is not on record.
- **What is wrong: the failure list drops more than it needs to.** `REFUSED_FAILURES` drops markers that do not depend on the ACPI tables, which is more than D8's "the proofs that need the tables":
  - `(reserved) at rip`;
  - `did not keep its contents` and `could not be freed`;
  - `origin-kernel: no usable region can hold`;
  - `the processor has no NX bit` and `no code or read-only segment to probe`;
  - `APIC timer calibration failed` (the timer calibrates against the PIT).
- **What is wrong: the proofs merge the two UARTs.**
  - `UART_BYTES` counts data writes on both. So `prove_uart_sections`'s `written >= whole_line` and `prove_room`'s `written > needed + 1` pass when either UART alone got the line.
  - `emptied` and `report_out` look at COM1's queue only.
  - Both proofs return `true` at once when COM1 has been given up, because `uart_is_on` asks about COM1 only, whatever state the loader's UART is in.
- **Failing case:** a fault in the loader's queue under contention, such as lines interleaved or one lost on COM2 alone.
  - `console_lines_intact` would find it on COM2, but the refused machine does not run that check.
  - The proofs count COM1's bytes and pass.
  - A console proof that fails only with two UARTs live prints `: console failed`, and the scenario still passes.
- **Fix:**
  - Add a machine on which only the SPCR is in loader data (an `spcr_in_ram` flag for the TEST ONLY application, with the XSDT copy pointing at it):
    - the loader reads it under boot services and names COM2;
    - the kernel cannot read it (`read_firmware` refuses RAM), so it refuses the move;
    - every other table stays readable.
    - Hold it to the full `boot_failures()` and to the rented machine's integrity checks, on COM1 and on COM2. The census's line about the one unreadable entry will probably need holding by name.
  - On the machine with the RSDP in RAM:
    - derive the failure list from `boot_failures()` minus a named list of markers that depend on the tables;
    - hold the verdict's failed and not-run proofs to that list.
  - In the proofs:
    - count bytes per UART;
    - check every live UART's queue;
    - do not return early while the loader's UART is live.

### T84-4 (Medium): "nothing is sent again" and "COM1 and COM2 carry the same lines" are checked only by the absence of the replay's sentence

- **Where:**
  - xtask `main.rs`: 4291-4306 (`check_spcr_console`), 4310-4325 (`check_first_line_on_the_uart`), and 2825-2830 (`check_refused_boot`'s test for `REPLAYED`).
  - TASKS §84 D8 and `STATUS.md`: "COM1 and COM2 carried the same 321 kernel lines".
- **What is wrong:**
  - The scenarios check three things:
    - the first two kernel lines on COM2;
    - that ` bytes of earlier output sent to it again` does not appear;
    - the markers, in order.
  - A fault that sends the early log again without that sentence, or that loses or duplicates lines on one UART, passes.
  - The "same 321 lines" in the records was observed by hand, not checked.
- **Failing case:**
  - A well-meant edit adds `emit(&mut line.uart, sinks.early.as_bytes())` to `take_over_loader_uart` before `early.clear()`.
  - COM2 then carries every line from before the move twice.
  - Its first two kernel lines are still the framebuffer's and the line about the loader's UART, the replay's sentence never appears, and the markers are found in order.
  - So `boot-console` and `boot-rented` pass. The same holds for a line lost on COM2 before the move.
- **Fix:**
  - In `check_spcr_console`, COM2's kernel lines up to and including the take-over line must equal COM1's kernel lines exactly: the same lines, in the same order, each once.
  - In `check_refused_boot`, the two UARTs' kernel lines must be equal for the whole boot. The only difference allowed is the queue's "some lines were lost here" notice, where a writer that cannot wait lost a line on one UART.
  - That turns D8's observation into a check, and catches duplicates and losses on either UART.

### T84-5 (Medium): no boot now moves the console to another UART and replays its log, and the new branch that drops a live loader's UART after such a move never runs

- **Where:**
  - xtask `main.rs`: 4247-4289 (`spcr_uart_scenario`, now a take-over), 2839-2900 (`rented_scenario`, the same), and 4352-4480 (the other console scenarios).
  - `console.rs:1249-1316` (`move_uart`): the replay, and the new lines 1309-1314.
- **What is wrong:** before item 84, the SPCR console scenario and `boot-rented` booted E4's whole move: a new I/O-port UART, the early log replayed, COM1 stopping. Now both take the take-over instead.
  - **What the other scenarios do:**
    - The scenario naming COM1 itself does not replay: it is the same port, and "0 bytes" are sent again.
    - The two scenarios at `0x2E8` fail or refuse the move.
    - The scenario naming registers in RAM is refused.
  - **What no boot runs any more:**
    - `move_uart`'s successful path for another port;
    - the replay's `emit`;
    - `MOVED_UART` set to the port moved to;
    - the new `sinks.loader = None`.
  - **Why it matters:** this path is still E4's only route to the memory-mapped LPSS and FCH UARTs of the declared 2026 platforms (ADR-0009), which QEMU cannot boot. The I/O-port replay was the mechanism's only boot evidence.
- **Failing case:** say a fault in `move_uart` replays from the cleared queue, or no longer sets `MOVED_UART`. It passes `check`, `boot-test`, `boot-console` and `boot-rented`.
- **Fix:**
  - Add a console scenario whose SPCR the loader does not name but the kernel does: an SPCR longer than `MAX_SPCR_LENGTH` (1,024 bytes), for example revision 4 with a long namespace string.
    - The loader then says `it declares N bytes, more than the loader reads (1024)`: D4's case, which no boot runs today.
    - The kernel moves to `0x2F8` and replays.
    - Keep the old markers (`moving the console there`, `N bytes of earlier output sent to it again`) and the old move line for `check_com1_stopped_at_the_move`.
    - With T84-8's fix, a first SPCR that is too long still names nothing, so this scenario is unaffected.
  - For `sinks.loader = None`, a host test with mock UARTs is enough.

### T84-6 (Medium): the loader reads memory the kernel's walk refuses to read: MMIO, unaccepted memory and the null page

- **Where:**
  - `boot/uefi/src/spcr.rs:121-146`: `described` accepts any kind of region.
  - `boot/uefi/src/main.rs:890-902`: `read_described`.
  - Compare the kernel:
    - `framekernel/frame/src/firmware.rs:33-35`, `:79-81` and `:93` (`names_mmio`: "the memory map calls it MMIO, which holds no table", TASKS item 71 F14);
    - `framekernel/frame/src/vm.rs:619` (`is_firmware_data`: firmware kinds only, and "Unaccepted memory is RAM, not firmware data").
- **What is wrong:** the contract asks for the kernel's checks ("as the kernel's walk is"). The loader accepts:
  - any range the map describes, including `RegionKind::Mmio` (UEFI types 11 and 12) and `Unaccepted` (type 15);
  - any address except exactly 0.

  Its reads are volatile byte loads.
- **Failing cases:**
  1. **MMIO.** An XSDT entry, or the RSDP's XSDT address, points into an `EfiMemoryMappedIO` range: a corrupt table, or a stale pointer after a firmware update. The loader reads 36 bytes of device registers, where a read can pop a FIFO or clear a status bit. The kernel refuses the same address by name.
  2. **Page 0.** An entry such as `0x10`, on firmware that unmaps page 0 to detect null pointers. The map describes the range, since page 0 is usually boot-services or conventional memory. The read faults in the loader, and the firmware's exception handler stops the boot, against security invariant 3.
  3. **Unaccepted memory.** In a confidential guest, reading unaccepted private memory raises #VE or #VC in the loader.
- **Fix:**
  - Let `described` use the kinds it already has. Refuse any range that touches `Mmio` or `Unaccepted`, or that starts below 4 KiB.
  - Do not narrow it to the kernel's firmware-only kinds: the refused machine's RSDP is deliberately in loader data.
  - Add the three cases to `only_described_memory_is_read`.

### T84-7 (Medium): the loader's mutation soak cannot fail on what it says it checks

- **Where:** `boot/uefi/src/spcr/tests.rs:403-442` (`hostile_tables_never_name_an_unchecked_uart`), cited as acceptance evidence by `docs/requirements/adr-0010.md:15` (A10-Am3).
- **What is wrong:** its documentation says the walk "never reads outside the model's memory, and names a UART only when boot info's own check accepts it". Neither part is checked:
  1. **Reads.** `Memory::read` returns `false` for a read outside the model, and nothing records or asserts that one happened.
  2. **The UART.** `Found::Port` is built only in `spcr_at`, after `uart.check()` returned `Ok`. So `assert_eq!(uart.check(), Ok(()))` cannot fail, and `port_block_allowed(uart.port)` is the very function `check` calls.
  - A fault in the shared port rule (R84-3's kind of fault) therefore passes both assertions. What the soak does test is that the walk never panics.
- **Why Medium and not High:** the property is enforced in code the soak cannot get around. Two other tests check it with independent rules:
  - the literal list in `an_early_uart_is_checked_field_by_field`, which caught R84-3;
  - `origin-spcr`'s soak, whose rule is the literal port blocks.

  The gap is the soak's stated claim, and its use as evidence.
- **Failing case:** replace `port_block_allowed`'s body with `port >= 0x40`. This soak still passes its 20,000 cases; R84-3's host test catches the change.
- **Fix:**
  - Assert against a literal rule: the four COM bases, or `0x1000..=0xFFF8`, as `origin-spcr`'s soak does.
  - Have `Memory::read` record every request.
  - Assert that each request lies inside one of the model's tables and within the length the walk had checked, apart from the deliberate unreadable cases.
  - Count the refused reads.

### T84-8 (Low): the loader's walk departs in two cases from the kernel's rule of the first SPCR whose header checks

- **Where:** `boot/uefi/src/spcr.rs:169-198` (`walk`) and `263-272`. In `spcr_at`, `TooLong` and `Unreadable` return `Err`, so the walk goes on. Compare `framekernel/frame/src/acpi.rs:906`, where `find_table` skips null entries.
- **What is wrong:**
  1. **An SPCR the loader cannot check.** A table over 1 KiB, or one whose body the loader cannot read, is treated like a header that did not check. The loader goes on and may name a later SPCR. The kernel reads the first one (up to 16 MiB) and, if its header checks, looks no further. D4 says "a longer SPCR names no UART", yet the walk can still name another table's UART.
  2. **A null XSDT entry.** The loader treats it as `EntryUnreadable(0x0)` and says "the XSDT lists none readable; the table at 0x0 is not in memory the firmware's map describes". The kernel treats it as naming nothing (`NOT_LISTED`).
- **Failing case:**
  - An XSDT lists an SPCR over 1 KiB naming `0x2F8` with interface 3, which is refused, then a normal SPCR naming `0x3E8`.
  - The loader names `0x3E8`, and the kernel's move refuses the first table.
  - By ruling Q1, the kernel then writes to `0x3E8` for the whole boot, a UART its own reading never named.
  - The port passed both checks, so no invariant breaks; the result simply departs from the contract.
  - Firmware with a null entry and no SPCR gets a misleading line from the loader.
- **Fix:**
  - Stop the walk at the first entry signed `SPCR` that the loader cannot check in full (`TooLong`, `Unreadable`), and name no UART, giving that reason.
  - Skip null entries, as the kernel does.

### T84-9 (Low): the loader's UART is given up on with weaker guarantees than the console's own

- **Where:**
  - `console.rs:476-489`: `give_up_on_loader_uart`.
  - `console.rs:1029-1035`: `configure`, called in `attach_loader_uart`.
  - `console.rs:940-990`: `LoaderUart`'s line.
  - `console.rs:1082-1085`: the order in which the take-over clears `LOADER_UART_ON` and sets `MOVED_UART`.
- **What is wrong:**
  - **The notice.** `the loader's UART stopped taking bytes` is queued behind the line being sent, and `stop_notice` is not set. The writer that found the stop returns before the notice has gone out or been drawn. Amendment 2 holds the console's own UART to more: "the writer that found it out goes on only when that line has been drawn, even if nothing is printed again". If the kernel stops right after, the notice stays queued.
  - **Two waits.** `attach_loader_uart` calls `configure`, which waits up to `TX_POLL_LIMIT` polls for the transmitter to empty. The first line's pump then waits again. A UART that never empties costs two bounded waits, where D3 says one.
  - **A stale line.** When that UART is given up on during the first line, the next line still says `written to from the first line, beside COM1, …`. The "takes no bytes" scenario's marker expects exactly that.
  - **A window.** `take_over_loader_uart` clears `LOADER_UART_ON` before it sets `MOVED_UART`. A lock-free report in between goes to COM1 only. Only an NMI or a machine check can arrive there.
- **Fix:**
  - Give the notice a position the writer waits for, as `stop_notice` does.
  - Skip `configure`'s wait, or count it as the one wait.
  - Have `LoaderUart`'s line say that the UART stopped taking bytes when it is no longer alive.
  - Set `MOVED_UART` before clearing `LOADER_UART_ON`.

### T84-10 (Low): when the loader names COM1, the kernel keeps its own 115,200 baud until the move

- **Where:** `console.rs:1006-1008` (`LoaderUart::Com1`); `kernel.rs`'s `uart.init()`, before `console::init`.
- **What is wrong:**
  - The objective is that every line reaches the SPCR's UART, readably, from the first line.
  - For COM1, the kernel reprograms the UART to 115,200 baud 8N1 at entry.
  - `attach_loader_uart` then leaves it there. It does not apply the SPCR's speed rule, as `move_uart` does for the same port: `line_setup` with the firmware's latch, or `restore(firmware_line)`.
- **Failing case:**
  - A server's console is COM1 at 9,600 baud: the SPCR's configured rate is code 3, and the firmware's latch holds 12.
  - Every line before the move is garbage on the operator's terminal.
  - A kernel that stops before the move is as silent there as rental B's was on COM2.
  - The move later restores the speed and replays, so a boot that reaches the move is unaffected.
- **Fix:**
  - In the `Com1` case, apply `origin_spcr::line_setup(Port(0x3F8), stated_baud, stated_clock, firmware_line.divisor)`.
  - Restore the firmware's line for `Keep`, as `move_uart`'s same-port branch does.
  - The move then finds nothing left to change.

### T84-13 (Low): a second implementation of the ACPI header's length and checksum rules

- **Where:** `firmware/acpi/spcr/src/lib.rs:40-58` (`check`). Compare `firmware/acpi/tables/src/header.rs:94-112`, and `framekernel/frame/src/acpi.rs`'s `check_table`, whose comment says the rules live in one place "so the tree holds one implementation of them".
- **What is wrong:** the two agree today (a declared length of at least 36 bytes, within the bytes given, summing to zero). But the loader and the kernel now check an SPCR's header with different code, which the contract's "the same parser" was meant to avoid.
- **Fix:**
  - Have the loader call `origin_acpi_tables::header::check_signature(whole, b"SPCR")`. That crate depends on nothing, so ruling Q3 still holds for `origin-spcr`.
  - Otherwise, document `origin_spcr::check` as a copy, and test it against the original.

### T84-14 (Informational): D1, the loader's line under QEMU, is sound

- **What addition (b) asked:** the loader's line naming `0x2F8`, and the kernel's refusal, both on COM2.
- **Why the literal ask cannot be met:** under OVMF the loader's text goes through the firmware's console, and OVMF writes it to the UART at `0x3F8` whatever QEMU's serial devices are. Getting it onto COM2 would need either of two things:
  - an OVMF built with another serial base: firmware the project does not pin;
  - the loader writing to the UART itself: a second writer of that hardware in the loader, and every line twice on a board like rental B's, whose firmware console is that UART.
- **Why the substitute is sound:** it checks both facts where the operator looks.
  - The kernel's second line on COM2 states the port and interface that boot info carried. So it proves the hand-over the loader made, not only what the loader meant to do.
  - The refusal is on COM2.
  - The loader's own line is checked on COM1.
  - No better option is available at this cost.
- **Three strengthenings:**
  - With T84-4, check that COM2's kernel lines equal COM1's, so that the refusal on COM2 is the very same line.
  - Parse the port from the loader's line on COM1 and from the kernel's line on COM2, and compare them. Today both are xtask constants, so they agree by construction.
  - Record the coordinating session's acceptance of D1, since D1 replaces an addition that session asked for.

### T84-15 (Informational): the loader's walk of a 16 MiB XSDT

- **Where:** `spcr.rs:25-26`, `:169-193` and `:227-258`.
- **The cost:** `MAX_XSDT_LENGTH` is the kernel's 16 MiB. A checksummed XSDT of that size, in described memory, costs the loader, under boot services:
  - 262,144 chunk reads;
  - up to about 2.1 million entry reads, and as many 36-byte header reads, each through `described`'s scan of the map;
  - in all, a second or more.
- **Why this is informational:** the cost is bounded, and the firmware is trusted for its tables. Still, the loader could take a much smaller limit, as it does for the SPCR (1 KiB), since the kernel reads the table itself anyway. No real XSDT comes near 64 KiB.

## Checked and found sound

- **ABI layout.**
  - `EarlyUart` is 16 bytes, 4-byte aligned, with no padding.
  - `BootInfo` is 200 bytes: `early_uart` at 184, 8-byte aligned, with no padding at the end.
  - Every offset is pinned by a compile-time assert.
  - The loader sizes the boot-info region from `size_of::<BootInfo>()`.
  - The boot seed's clear-of rule uses the new size, and its test moved with it (`0x80C7`, `0x80C8`, `0x8110`).
- **Every literal `BootInfo`** carries `early_uart`: the loader, `boot/info`'s tests, `framekernel/frame/src/boot.rs`'s tests and `report.rs`'s tests.
- **Version.**
  - `origin-version.toml` and `runtime/version/src/generated.rs` both say 0.8.
  - The major-0 rule wants an exact match, so 0.7 is refused (R84-5).
  - `size` below 200 is refused.
  - The loader refuses a kernel built for another version (`boot/uefi/src/kernel.rs:29`).
- **`EarlyUart::check`:**
  - `NONE` exactly when the port is 0;
  - the port through the parser's own `port_block_allowed`;
  - the interface through `is_16550`;
  - the flags, rate, clock and reserved word consistent;
  - tested field by field against a literal list.
- **The parser moved unchanged.**
  - `parse` differs from the kernel's copy at `9517b2f6` by the revision offset's constant and the `is_16550` call.
  - `divisor_for` and `LineSetup` are identical.
  - The free `line_setup` is the old method's rule.
- **`origin_spcr::check`:**
  - a declared length of at least 36 bytes and no more than the bytes given;
  - the signature;
  - the checksum over the declared length;
  - it returns only that prefix.
- **`described` cannot accept memory the map does not cover.**
  - It moves on only from a region with `base <= at < end`, so no input order can make it accept what the map does not cover: unsorted, overlapping or merged regions give at most a false refusal.
  - It refuses ranges that wrap, start at 0 or are empty.
  - `memmap::translate` sorts the map, refuses overlaps and merges neighbours anyway.
- **Every loader read is checked first and bounded.**
  - Each goes through `read_described`, which runs `described` before its first byte, and reads byte by byte, volatile, holding no reference.
  - The bounds: the RSDP's 36 bytes; an XSDT of at most 16 MiB, summed in 64-byte chunks; entries inside the summed length; 36-byte headers; an SPCR read into a 1 KiB buffer.
  - The SPCR is read a second time, and `check` holds the declared length it finds then to the bytes actually read, so a table that changed between the reads cannot overrun.
  - No slice is taken without `get` or `get_mut`: no read panics.
- **The walk matches the kernel's** on the RSDP (signature, the 20-byte sum, revision 2 or later, length at least 36, the extended sum over 36 bytes), on the XSDT (signature, length bounds, whole-table sum), and on the rule that an SPCR whose header checks decides, whatever the parser says. The two exceptions are in T84-6 and T84-8.
- **Nothing stops the loader.** Every outcome of the walk is a `Found`, and a memory map that cannot be read names no UART.
- **Security invariants 1, 2 and 4.**
  - The loader runs the parser, then `EarlyUart::check`.
  - The kernel runs `EarlyUart::check` before it claims, probes or writes.
  - The ports are claimed through `PortRange::claim`, which refuses the system's ports and any overlap.
  - The lock-free report path writes to `LOADER_UART` only while `LOADER_UART_ON` is set, which happens only after the check and the probe pass.
- **The ports' claim.**
  - It is kept in `LOADER_UART` for good, since `PortRange::claim` never releases, so no driver can claim those ports.
  - `KernelUart::port` hands the same claim to the move.
  - `uart_for` always probes.
  - The take-over reuses the UART probed at attach.
- **`reached` never holds a writer for ever.**
  - A UART given up on, whose queue was cleared with `sent = queued`, holds no writer.
  - `send_and_draw` and `send_report` loop only while `pump_uart` reports bytes waiting, and each queue either moves or reaches `Stopped` within its bounded wait.
- **Positions across the take-over.**
  - The queues are swapped with the lock held and interrupts off.
  - The swap comes before the timer and before any other processor starts: `kernel.rs:186` against `:229` and `:297`.
  - The boot flow's own line has finished by then.
  - So no position in COM1's old queue is still being waited for (T84-1 aside).
- **`send` keeps one order.**
  - It pushes to COM1's queue, the loader's queue and the screen in one critical section, so all three get the lines in one order.
  - A writer that can wait gets `NoRoom` when either UART's queue is full, and `make_room` pumps it.
- **The urgent path without the lock** writes to COM1, or to `MOVED_UART`, plus the loader's UART while it is live. After a take-over, `MOVED_UART` is the loader's UART, so nothing is written twice.
- **The refused machine checks that writes are timely at the end of the boot.** Its verdict and halt lines must be on COM2, and nothing pumps the queues after `halt()`. Control R84-4 shows that this check can fail.
- **Every ordinary boot now carries both new lines as markers:** `LOADER_NO_SPCR` and `KERNEL_NO_LOADER_UART`. The five other SPCR scenarios' markers match D3, D7 and the code.
- **D2, D3, D5 and D7** describe the code as built.
- **The gates learned the new crate:**
  - `FREESTANDING_LIBS`, so the no-panic check covers it;
  - `KERNEL_LEAVES` and `LAYERS`;
  - `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` and no dependencies in the crate itself.
- **Settings.**
  - `ORIGIN_QEMU_SHAPE` is parsed case-sensitively.
  - `refused` together with `RESERVED`, or on a processor without RDSEED and RDRAND, is refused.
  - The commands that set their own machines refuse it.
  - The TEST ONLY application's configuration version 4 refuses version 3 and unknown flags.
- **Controls R84-1 to R84-5** each break the behaviour they are named for, and the recorded first missing markers follow from the code.
