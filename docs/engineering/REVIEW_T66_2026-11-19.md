## T66-R1 (High): the preemption proof (Refuse) now passes a kernel whose tick on a processor is slow or masked, in the disturbed run itself, with no repeat and no evidence test

**Where:**
- `framekernel/src/sched.rs:1098-1105`: `WINDOW_TICKS`, `WINDOW_BUDGET_SECONDS`.
- `framekernel/src/sched.rs:1212`: the window loop.
- `framekernel/src/sched.rs:1218-1231`: the window's line, "judged by nothing".
- `framekernel/src/sched.rs:1265-1267`: the verdict, `switches >= 15` with no time term.

**What changed:**
- **Before:** each processor had to switch 15 times within 500 ms of TSC time.
- **Now:** the window lasts until every processor has taken 50 of its own ticks, up to 10 s. The verdict is still only "15 switches and both workers ran", whenever they happened.
- **Measured in what it tests:** the window is counted in the very ticks whose delivery the proof exists to check. Nothing compares a processor's ticks with the TSC.
- **No evidence test:** nothing tells a deschedule from a wrong rate or from interrupts held off.
- **No repeat:** the disturbed run itself passes.
- **The budget line decides nothing:** even "reached its 10 s budget with CPU n short of 50 of its own ticks" is not a failure. Such a processor passes if it switched 15 times in those 10 s, which needs only about 1.5 Hz.

**Failing scenarios:**
1. **The task's own control T66-9** (ADR-0013:633; log `controls1/T66-9.log`).
   - A worker on CPU 1 holds interrupts off for 400 ms. That is the brief's "interrupts held off by a kernel bug".
   - It passes: "the preemption window lasted 900 ms … CPU 1: … 52 context switches", then "preemptive multitasking: … all ran".
   - Under the old 500 ms window, CPU 1 would have had about 10 ticks of its own, so fewer than 15 switches, and the boot would have been refused.
   - ADR-0013:645 files this control under "each disturbance short of the budget was repeated and passed". Nothing was repeated: the run with the fault in it passed.
2. **A wrong rate in `timer::start_periodic`** (`frame/src/timer.rs:342`).
   - Suppose a bug there wrote divide-by-128 instead of 16, or a wrong count from `PERIOD`. Every processor's scheduler tick would run at 12.5 Hz.
   - R5's rate proof would not catch it. `take_ticks` (`timer.rs:573`) never calls `start_periodic`: it relies on the divide set during calibration.
   - **Before:** about 6 switches in 500 ms, so Preemption failed and the boot was refused.
   - **Now:** 50 ticks in 4 s, under the 10 s budget, 50 switches. The boot passes, degraded only by Report rows. The timed waits on `far` repeat on "lost ticks" until run 8 and then fail as Report.
   - Any processor's tick between about 1.5 Hz and 30 Hz behaves the same way. Before, the floor was about 30 Hz.
   - No other proof bounds a processor's tick rate. SIMD and lifecycle run on the boot processor; weights and the timed waits run on `far` only, and are Report.

**What this contradicts:**
- The brief's rule: "evidence of a disturbance buys a repeat against the same bound, never a looser verdict".
- The contract's security invariant (1), `TASKS.md:2178`: "Every run that passes meets today's bound".
- ADR-0013:606: "No real-machine bound … changed".
- ADR-0013's own Preemption row (line 102) admits "only a tick that does not come fails it".
- **On bare metal:** the only trace is "lasted N ms", which nothing judges.

**Suggested fix:** keep 15 switches within 500 ms of TSC time as the bound, and earn repeats by evidence, as R3 and R5 do.
- **What earns a repeat:** a processor short of switches that also lost at least 3 of the 50 ticks the window should hold (`TICKS_LOST_TO_A_STALL`). Measure again with fresh workers, up to N runs, and say `run R of N (after a stalled run)`.
- **What fails at once:** a processor short of switches with its ticks all there.
- **The rate:** add a Report clause, or a repeat rule, so that a processor whose 50 ticks took over 750 ms of TSC time without merged-expiry evidence fails. For example, have the tick handler track each processor's longest tick gap, and use R5's median-gap test.
- **Controls to add:**
  - `start_periodic` with a wrong divisor on the APs. It must fail.
  - T66-9 again. It must be repeated, not passed in the disturbed run.

**Confidence:** high, from the code, the task's own T66-9 log and arithmetic. The divisor scenario was not booted.

## T66-R2 (Medium): R3's "boot flow away" evidence for the signalled wait is mis-measured, and it has no one-processor guard

**Where:**
- `framekernel/src/wait.rs:293`: `sched::yield_until_away(|| false, tsc_hz / 50, 1)`.
- `framekernel/src/sched.rs:743-744`: `limit = (tsc_hz / 1_000_000).max(1) * RUNNING_STEP_MICROS`.
- `framekernel/src/wait.rs:341`: `judge_timed_waits(…, run.away, hz)`.
- `framekernel/src/runqueue.rs:148-149`: the long wait excused by `away_per_mille >= DISTURBED_PER_MILLE`.

**The mis-measurement:**
- The first phase passes `tsc_hz / 50` as the rate, so the 20 ms duration comes out right.
- `yield_until_away` derives its "away" threshold from that same argument. So in the first phase a step counts as away from 3 ms / 50 = **60 µs**, not 3 ms (`RUNNING_STEP_MICROS` = 3000).
- That phase's away is added to the second phase's and divided by the whole run, about 50 to 80 ms.
- Any loop iteration of 60 µs to 3 ms in the first 20 ms counts as absence. That covers a `reap()` that reclaims a stack with a shootdown, a tick handler, or a switch to another thread. Such steps are common under TCG and on the large machines.
- About 5 to 8 ms of such steps reach the 100 ‰ that excuses a late signalled wait.

**The missing guard:**
- The ping-pong zeroes `away` when `far == bsp` (`wait.rs:466`). Its documentation says a run on one processor "fails at once", because the threads on that processor make the reading themselves.
- R3 has no such guard. On a one-processor machine the waiters run on the boot flow's own processor.

**Failing scenario:**
- A kernel bug delays the wake of the signalled waiter, for example a wake IPI handled only at the next tick-driven recheck about 600 ms later. Its processor's ticks are all there and the boot flow was present.
- Per the contract, that should fail at once.
- Instead it can be judged `Repeat` on spurious away and measured up to 8 times. An intermittent wake fault then gets 8 chances to pass.
- Because the timed-wait line does not print `away`, no log can show why a run was repeated.
- Repeats still never pass a late run. A persistent fault fails at run 8, which is why this is not High.

**Confidence:**
- The arithmetic is certain from the code.
- How often it changes a verdict is not measured, since the line does not print `away`.

**Suggested fix:**
- Measure the 20 ms phase with the real `tsc_hz`. For example, give `yield_until_away` a duration in ticks instead of reusing the rate argument.
- Zero `away` when `far == bsp`, as the ping-pong does.
- Print the away figure on the timed-wait line.
- Add a control: the signalled wait late with its ticks present and the boot flow present must fail at run 1.

## T66-R4 (Low): `apic::judge_ticks` misjudges a run given more stamps than `wanted`

**Where:** `framekernel/frame/src/apic.rs:283-318`.

**The defect:** it uses up to `MAX_TICK_STAMPS` (64) stamps for `elapsed`, but compares against the nominal time of `wanted`.

**Probe results:**
- 64 stamps at 75 % of the period with `wanted = 50` gives `InBounds`, though the rate is 25 % fast.
- 64 stamps at 120 % gives `Wrong`.

**Reach:** not reachable today, because the kernel passes at most 50 stamps (`timer.rs:513-518`). It is a hazard in a public function whose documentation invites up to 64.

**Fix:** truncate to `wanted` (`stamps.len().min(wanted).min(MAX_TICK_STAMPS)`), and add a test.

## T66-R5 (Low): the `InBounds` verdict ignores the median the function now computes, so a fast tick masked by a stall still passes

**Where:** `framekernel/frame/src/apic.rs:301-304`.

**Probe:** a run at 2× the programmed rate with a 300 ms stall gives `InBounds`, with `median_gap` at 50 % of the period.

**Reach:** this gap existed before, since the old code judged only the total. But `TooFast`'s documentation ("a stall can only make interrupts later, so this fails at once") and the contract's "a wrong divisor … moves the median and fails at once" claim more than the code does.

**Fix:** require the median within 95 % to 150 % for `InBounds` as well.

**Caveat seen in the evidence:** under TCG the median can sit about 10 % below the true period. T66-11 had a median of 18.1 ms for a 20 ms period. So a slow rate of up to about 1.65× can be judged `Stalled` (a repeat) rather than `Wrong`; it still fails at run 4.

## T66-R6 (Low): a stale pending timer interrupt can carry into R5's repeated run and loosen its slow bound to about 153 %

**Where:**
- `framekernel/frame/src/timer.rs:573-598` (`take_ticks`);
- `framekernel/frame/src/timer.rs:171-177` (`stop_timer` masks the LVT and zeroes the count but cannot withdraw an interrupt already in the IRR).

**The scenario:**
- A `Stalled` run that ends at its 2 s limit can leave one expiry pending.
- The next run counts it at its first interrupt window, after `before` is read.
- That run then needs only 49 real periods for its 50 stamps.
- Probe: a stale first stamp plus 49 gaps at 153 % gives `InBounds`; 50 gaps at 153 % give `Wrong`.

**Reach:** the window for this is short (between the last poll and `stop_timer`). It still breaks "never a looser verdict" for repeated runs.

**Fix:** drain the pending interrupt after stopping the timer before the next run (one interrupt window), or discard any stamp seen before the first full period.

## T66-R7 (Low): long and droppable diagnostic lines

**Where:**
- `framekernel/frame/src/timer.rs:530-543`;
- `framekernel/src/sched.rs:209-227`;
- `framekernel/src/console.rs` (`LINE` = 160; `Flow::NoWaiting`).

**The problems:**
- **R5's failure line is about 163 to 165 bytes,** and `: APIC timer is wrong` straddles byte 160, where the console splits a line into pieces. Today nothing else writes during `timer::init`, so the pieces stay together. That is safe by accident.
- **R5's failure line for a short run prints the time to the last interrupt, not the run's length.** With no interrupt at all it prints "0 interrupts in 0 us".
- **The reaper's line is 164 to 166 bytes,** so it always goes out in two pieces. It is printed from processors that run while the boot flow prints, so another line can land between its pieces.
- **From the boot flow the reaper's line can be dropped.** `yield_until_away` calls `reap()` with interrupts off, and `Console::new()` there is `NoWaiting`, which drops the line when the UART queue is full.

**Fix:**
- Keep both lines under 160 bytes.
- Print the run's length for a short run.
- Use the boot-flow console when called from the boot flow.

## T66-R8 (Low): R8 reports less than the records say, and its required host tests are missing

**Where:**
- `framekernel/src/sched.rs:196-203` and `:245`;
- `framekernel/src/stacks.rs:171,309,390`;
- `framekernel/frame/src/tlb.rs:214-275`;
- ADR-0013:604;
- `TASKS.md:2180`.

**Coverage:**
- Only `sched::reap`'s reclaims print the line.
- Nothing prints for:
  - a stack lost in `settle`'s own second `stack::reclaim()` (sched.rs:245);
  - stacks lost in the stacks proof's reclaims;
  - user-space (`shootdown_space`) stalls.
- These are counted only in the next printed line's "stall N of this boot".

**Attribution:** the line names `last_stall()`, which can be a different stall than the one that lost the stack. The code comment admits this.

**Tests:** the contract's required tests include "Host tests … of R8's records". There are none, since `frame/src/tlb.rs` builds only for the kernel's target, and the departure is not recorded.

**Fix:** call `report_lost_stacks` wherever a reclaim's `lost` rises; move `note`/`last_stall` into a host-testable type with tests; or record the narrower scope.

## T66-R9 (Low): R6 lengthens an interrupts-off spin on the boot processor beyond the shootdown patience

**Where:**
- `framekernel/src/time.rs:615-645` (`take_turns`, called by the boot flow, which runs with interrupts off; no interrupt window inside);
- `framekernel/src/time.rs:546` and `:719`.

**The change:** the boot processor can now spin with interrupts off for up to 5 s (before: 0.5 s). That is beyond the 1 s shootdown patience.

**What would go wrong:** any processor that started a shootdown during a long run would stall on the boot processor. For a stack reclaim that withholds the stack, and `tlb::shootdown` panics. I found no issuer: every other processor runs an always-ready turn-taker, so its idle reaper does not run.

**Status:** this is a latent hazard. I could not show a failure.

**Fix:** open an interrupt window between turns on the boot flow, or keep the cap under the patience.

## Checked and found sound

- **R5's judgement** (`apic.rs:283-318`, `timer.rs:504-566`):
  - `Stalled` never passes. A run judged `Stalled` at run 4 fails.
  - A uniformly wrong slow rate moves the median and is `Wrong` at once. This held at every grid point and in my probes; for example 25×P + 25×3P gives `Wrong`.
  - Exactly 95 % and 150 % pass.
  - The upper median of an even count is the conservative choice. 26×P + 24×3P gives `Stalled`, and fails at run 4 if persistent.
  - One or two stamps give `Wrong`.
  - No overflow: the arithmetic saturates and is bounded.
  - The pass line always says `50 interrupts`, which removes F5's marker artefact.
  - **Every-run faults fail at run 4:** a periodic slow handler or merged expiries in every run (T66-13).
  - **The evidence's independence:** it is the boot processor's own TSC at its own polls. At this point no other kernel code runs on that processor besides the proof and its handler. A first-run-only fault (for example a one-time 300 ms interrupts-off stretch) now passes on run 2. That is the accepted trade, and the pass line says `run 2 of 4 (after a stalled run: a gap of …)`.
- **R3's judgement** (`runqueue.rs:133-152`, `wait.rs:283-392`):
  - `Pass` only when 50 ms ≤ short ≤ 80 ms and long < 500 ms. This held over the author's grid and my edge probes: 81/5 repeat, 89/6 fail, 400/5 repeat.
  - Early fails at once.
  - Correctness is judged on every run, and an unfinished run fails.
  - The ticks are the waiter's own processor's. The waiters are spawned on `far` and read `queue_ticks()` there.
  - The statics are reset per run, and the memory ordering is correct: `TIMED_TICKS` is written before the `TIMED` Release, which is read after the `FINISHED` Acquire.
  - Persistent masking or slow-tick faults fail at run 8 (T66-7).
  - **Limitation:** lost ticks are also the symptom of a kernel interrupt-masking bug or a wrong tick rate on `far`. They are not independent evidence. Only persistence and the bare-metal "(after a stalled run)" line separate the two.
- **R1:**
  - A "clean" window's brackets bound its error whatever the number of tries, so 1,024 tries add no false calibration.
  - A PIT that never reaches terminal count still leaves at the first window (`?` in `calibrate_pit`), so the 2^31-tick per-window budget is not multiplied by 1,024.
  - Windows with OUT2 stuck high are cheap port reads.
- **R2:**
  - Six edges is the most that 5.2 s holds.
  - The meter decides only on whole intervals, so more pairs cannot confirm a wrong rate.
  - A platform whose FADT says it has no CMOS RTC skips the measurement (`time.rs:205`), so it pays nothing more.
- **R7, deadlock:**
  - Every widened wait is `yield_until`, which checks its condition first and ends as soon as it holds.
  - The boot flow opens an interrupt window and calls `reap()` on every iteration, so it answers shootdowns.
  - No lock or object that the condition needs is held across the waits (`kernel_bound.rs:324-328` drops `process` first).
  - `Shootdown::shoot` services incoming requests while it waits.
  - The turn-takers spin with interrupts on.
  - I found no new wait-for cycle. A truly stuck partner now costs 10 s instead of 2 s before failing.
- **The checker:**
  - The only new lines carrying `: … failed` are real failures (`: wait timing failed` for the Report half; R5's `: APIC timer is wrong`).
  - `: wait timing failed` is in `boot_failures()` (`developer-tools/xtask/src/main.rs:2646`). It is used only where `correct` holds and the timing failed, for both the ping-pong (`ok` is timing only) and the timed waits. `wait queues failed` is kept for the Refuse half.
  - None of the new lines (the window lines, the reaper's line, R5's run suffix, the timed wait's suffix) contains an xtask marker literal; I grepped the evidence lines against xtask's literals.
  - `origin-kernel: stalled shootdown: ` matches no marker.
  - The window lines do not begin `origin-kernel: scheduler: CPU `, so the fair-share forgivers are unaffected.
  - No new line prints an address.
  - The markers `origin-kernel: APIC timer: 50 interrupts at 100 Hz in ` and the timed-wait fragments are still printed.
- **xtask:** no new allowance; the only change is the one failure entry.
- **`unsafe`:** no line containing `unsafe` changed in the diff.
- **The records' figures that I could check:**
  - ADR-0013's "host load of about 40" matches `summary.tsv` (39.6 to 46.3).
  - T66-9's 900 ms and the large machines' windows (780 to 1,329 ms) match the logs.
  - No record claims a KVM, CI or hardware result.
- **Host tests:** at `c0f0d02a`, `apic::tests`, `runqueue::tests` and `rtc::tests` pass, including the RTC meter's six-edge capacity test.
