## Checked and found sound

- **The bound is one step with the raise** **[code]**. In `reserve`, the check `used + reserved >= bound` and `reserved.fetch_add` are in one critical section under the port's lock. `reserved` is raised nowhere else. `full` is only a look; the real check is in `reserve`.
- **The `Relaxed` load is safe** **[inferred, C11 coherence]**. Every raise of `reserved` happens under the lock, so a load under the lock sees all earlier raises. The only writes it may miss are a dropped `Claim`'s decrements, made outside the lock. So the value read is at least the true count: a stale read can only refuse, never admit. Under contention a bind may be refused spuriously, which the ruling allows.
- **`reserved` never drifts or underflows** **[code]**. Each counted reservation is lowered exactly once: in `bind` under the lock (armed or queued, then `claim.counted = false`), or by `Claim::drop` when it is still counted. A refused bind hands the whole `Reservation` back, so its claim lowers it once. A reservation made for a closed port is never counted, and `bind` refuses it (`!counted`) without lowering.
- **The armed count cannot drift** **[code]**. It is `Index::used()`, `len − freed`, derived from the slots and not kept apart. `take` and `give_back` change `freed` only for the slot they change.
- **Every path that ends a binding gives its place back** **[code]**. Fire and implicit cancel (`Source::fire`, from `deliver` and `cancel_owners`): `give_back` under the port's lock. Explicit cancel: `give_back` under the port's lock if the port is open. Close: takes the whole index, so `used()` is 0. A failed allocation in `try_new` (the nodes, or the growth): nothing was raised. A dropped reservation: `Claim::drop`. A "re-arm" is a new bind with a new reservation.
- **The index grows only in `reserve`** **[code]**. `Index::take` pushes only below capacity; `grow` moves the slots with `append` inside room `bigger` already has, and keeps the larger block; `close` takes the vector; a growth that meets a closed port hands its block back.
- **The index's capacity stays within max(4, 2n − 1), even under races** **[code + inferred]**. On an open port, room is never less than `reserved`: room falls only at a bind, which lowers `reserved` with it. So when a look finds no room, room equals `reserved` and the capacity c equals `used + reserved`, which is under n. `wanted` is then the largest of `len + reserved + 1` (which is c + freed + 1, at most 2c + 1), 2c and 4. `grow` keeps the larger block. So every block ever installed has at most max(4, 2n − 1) slots. Only the old block's size (T1) needs a single grower. `try_reserve_exact` on an empty `Vec` gives exactly the capacity asked for (inferred; the author's host test holds it on the same `Vec` code).
- **The rule's two copies** **[code]**. `quota::index_at_bound` and `Port::index_at_bound` are the same formula, and the build compares them for 0 to 4,096 and for `usize::MAX`. Small bounds are conservative: at bound 2 the index never grows from 1 slot, yet 1 is counted.
- **The figure's arithmetic** **[code]**. `port_index` uses checked products and `footprint` for each block; zero gives 0; `usize::MAX` gives `None`, so `limit`. `Limits::parts` adds `costs.port` with a checked add and multiplies by `ports` through `times`. A binding's cost is `block(store) + block(ticket)`, with no slot. `Costs::index_slot` and `index_first` come from the framework's types.
- **The bound is the creator's** **[code]**. `port::create` is the only place the kernel makes a port. It reads `bindings_per_port` from the account it is given, which is the caller's own (`create_one` passes `p.process.account`). `KernelPort` keeps that account, so the creator's payment cannot come back while the port's memory exists. Its fields drop the port (whose index is already empty after `close`) before the account.
- **The order at a bind** **[code]**. The handles, the source, the binder's own `bindings` quota (`Binding::charge`), the port's bound (`full`, before the nodes), then memory. `Full` maps to `limit`, which `object_wait_async` declares; `NoMemory` to `no_memory`. The kernel's one caller of `try_new` matches the enum with no wildcard. A port bounded at 0 refuses at `full`, before allocating. A closed port, at any bound, still answers `bad_handle` (`full` is false for a closed port).
- **No default** **[code]**. `ObjectLimits` derives no `Default`. Every literal names `bindings_per_port` (the compiler requires it), and no literal uses `..` on an `ObjectLimits`. The generated program descriptions write no limits; `FIRST_PROGRAM_RUN` uses `NONE`.
- **Lock order** **[code]**. `full` takes only the port's lock, a leaf, from `try_new`, which runs with no lock held.
- **The Loom model and its control** **[code + inferred]**. `cargo xtask loom` finds the model by name in `models.rs` and requires that every model ran. The control's text occurs once in `ports.rs` (`full`'s comparison is formatted differently). With the check removed, one preemption after the first racer's `full` lets both reserve, and the model's assertion fails with the expected text. The model does exercise two growth blocks at once (T1); it does not look at memory.
- **The boot clause** **[code]**. The third bind's `limit` can come only from the port's bound: the binder holds 2 of its 4 `bindings`. Likewise the bound-0 refusal: the binder then holds 3. It would catch: no bound (KB-11), an off-by-one, the binder's account read in place of the creator's, a leaked `reserved` (the bind after the fire would be refused), a leaked charge, `no_memory` in place of `limit` (KB-14). It would not catch: a bound that counts only armed bindings (host, KB-12), a figure without the index (host, KB-13; T4), a check in `full` alone (Loom). The marker matches the printed line, and the failure word is already in `boot_failures`.

## Resolution

- **T1, `d4bdaf1`; Loom controls `two_growers_at_once` and `a_growth_wakes_no_one`.**
  - **The change.** `Port::reserve` looks under the port's lock. With no room and no growth under way, it sets the new `Queues::growing` mark and leaves to grow (`Port::grow`). With the mark set, it waits on the port's queue of waiters (`wait_until`, condition "the mark is clear", read under the port's lock), then looks again. `Port::grow` allocates with no lock held and installs the block under the lock, unless the port has closed. It frees what came back outside the lock, then takes the lock again to clear the mark, and wakes the queue. It clears the mark on every path: grown, refused by the heap, or the port closed.
  - **What follows.** The heap holds at most the index and the one block being made for it, or that block and the one it replaced, which held under n slots: the two blocks `quota::port_index` counts. Nothing was allocated under the port's spinlock.
  - **Why the wait ends and does not spin** (`Port::reserve`'s doc):
    - The grower holds no lock while it allocates, and nothing on its way to clearing the mark waits.
    - It never holds the port's lock and the queue's together. A waiter takes the queue's lock and then the port's, as a wait for a packet already does.
    - A waiter blocks, and the wait-queue protocol loses no wake-up. One that finds the queue full, or the boot flow, polls by yielding, as a wait for a packet does.
    - Each growth at least doubles the index, and an index grows only while it holds fewer slots than the bound. So a port grows at most about log2 of its bound times in its life, and one bind waits at most that many times.
    - A thread ends only by its own call or a fault in ring 3, never inside this one, so no grower is lost holding the mark.
  - **The cost.** A growth wakes the port's packet waiters too, which look again and wait again. The queue is shared rather than adding a second one, which would make every port larger in its creator's figure.
  - **The models.** `ports_two_reservations_race_for_a_bound_of_one` also checks the slots of every block alive at once against the figure, through a test-only count (`Port::most_index_slots`, `cfg(test)`). The new `ports_two_reservations_race_to_grow_a_bound_of_two` has both reservations hold a place, the waiter woken, and the same check.
  - **The controls.** `two_growers_at_once` (the mark ignored) is caught by both models: "the index's blocks at once passed what the figure counts". `a_growth_wakes_no_one` is caught by the second as a deadlock. `cargo xtask loom` on `597f174`: all 55 models passed and all 81 controls were caught; the two bound models ran 13,888 and 13,790 executions, at most 3 preemptions each.
  - **Records.** The rule's doc, K7, O11 (the item is gone: one growth at a time) and STATUS say that the replaced block is under n because one reservation grows at a time.
  - **Not modelled.** A third reservation waiting while a grower frees the replaced block and then clears its mark. That order rests on reading and the host tests (`models.rs`, "What is not proved").

- **T3, kept and recorded; `d4bdaf1` (`a_full_port_refuses_a_bind_that_would_queue_at_once`), `597f174` (`port::bind`'s doc).**
  - **The code that shows it.** `Port::reserve` raises `reserved` and keeps room for one slot, growing the index if it must. That place goes back only in `Source::bind`'s branch `if observed & request.mask != 0 { port.reserved.fetch_sub(1, Ordering::Relaxed); …; state.queue.push(stored); … }`, after the signals are read under the source's lock. So a bind that will queue at once holds a place from `Reservation::try_new` until then.
  - **Why the signals are read only there.** Reading them under the source's lock is what keeps a raise or a clear between a look and the bind from being missed (F2). Reading them before the bound would be a look the bind cannot rely on.
  - **Recorded** in K7, `port::bind`'s doc and A05-I1, as the same reading as the race.
