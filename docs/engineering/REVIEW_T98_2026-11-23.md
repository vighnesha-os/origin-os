## 1. The first review: `27699a39..a909ef26` and the pulled phases (the reviewer's text)

Read only: nothing was built, run or edited, so every finding below comes from reading the code. Paths are under the item's worktree.

I found no way for two processors to run one context, no lock-order fault and no lost wake-up. The findings are about liveness (S7's work-conserving claim), what the pulled proofs actually exercise, and several comments the code does not bear out.

### 1. Findings

#### R98-1. A processor outside the source's last-level cache never takes the single waiting thread: it is never judged cold
- **Severity:** defect (liveness, against S7's "no processor idles while another has two or more movable ready threads it may take"). No safety effect. Today only the proofs' threads are balanced.
- **Where:** `framekernel/src/sched/pull.rs` `tick_balanced` (569–580), `kick` (670–679), `pull_from` (426–434, 453); `framekernel/src/runqueue.rs` `judge` (1251), `cold` (1304).
- **Scenario:** S runs two equal-weight balanced threads T1 and T2 that never yield. Every idle processor P reaches S at a level with `shares_cache == false` (the two-package machines, or `CacheFrom::Nobody`).
  1. At each tick of S the step switches T1 to T2. `judge(quiet, was)` leaves out `was`, so `cold = {T1} & !bit(T1) = 0`.
  2. `kick` finds `lendable == 0` and sends nothing.
  3. Nothing calls into S between its ticks, so there is no later judgement.
  4. P's pass finds T1 not cold and not sharing the cache, counts `hot` and returns before the published check. So `unpublished == 0`, the result is `Pull::Nothing`, and no ask is sent.
  5. This repeats every tick. T1 has in fact been off the processor for up to 10 ms against H = 1 ms.
- **Wider form:** any voluntary switch on S since its mark makes `quiet` false at the next tick. A processor with one thread that blocks often never judges its long-waiting threads cold either.
- **Where it works:** three CPU-bound threads, or unequal weights, since a tick that switches nothing judges with `out == 0`.
- **Change:** an ask alone does not fix it. It arrives microseconds after S's tick, when `now - mark < H`. Two directions:
  - S judges before its step (the thread that waited the whole last tick is cold then, and is published once `publish_leaving_here` has run), and when it kicks an out-of-cache processor for it, keeps it out of that step's choice for one tick.
  - The idle processor looks a second time at least H after the source's tick.
  
  Either is a design decision for the ADR. A `RunQueue` host test with two alternating threads asserting that `cold()` eventually names the waiter would hold the fix.

#### R98-2. A pass can be captured by nearer victims it can never take from
- **Severity:** risk. Condition: three or more crowded processors that rank first for P and persistently give `Nothing`, or one that persistently gives `Asked`.
- **Where:** `pull.rs` `pass` (320–353: `tried` is per pass, `TRIES = 3`, `Pull::Asked => return false`); `framekernel/frame/sync/src/balance.rs` `choose` (397).
- **Scenario:** P is the only idle processor.
  - V1 to V3 rank first: each has two CPU-bound balanced threads P can never take (R98-1) and a higher published load, because pinned threads raise it.
  - V4 has a cold, published thread and kicks P every tick.
  - Each pass `choose` gives V1, V2, V3 in the same order, each `Nothing`, and P halts.
  - Every `Nothing` look republishes an honest census that still shows a surplus, so the order never changes. V4's thread waits while P idles, indefinitely.
- **Also:** `Asked` ends the pass even when the ask was not sent (`asks_over_rate`, `asks_not_first`), and without trying the next victim.
- **Change:**
  - Keep `tried`, or a rotating start, across passes until a pull succeeds.
  - Go on to the next victim after `Asked`, at least when nothing was sent.
  - Consider recording the kicker in the kicked processor's element, to be tried first.

#### R98-3. The pulled proofs do not exercise the new publication paths under the double-run detector, and no proof reads the bounds
- **Severity:** risk (a proof that can pass with the property broken).
- **Where:** `framekernel/src/migration_proof.rs` `integrity_thread` (621, whole loop with interrupts off), `integrity_by_pulls` (1537, `p.pulls() >= 1`), `delivery_by_pulls` (1622, `p.carrying() >= 1`); `pull.rs` `Counts`, `asks_taken` (238); `framekernel/frame/src/timer.rs` `balance_sent`.
- **a. Integrity threads never take a tick or an IPI where they run.**
  - Under the "running on" word and the canaries, the tick's kick, the reschedule upcall's kick and the ask's answer never act on a thread switched out in the same trap.
  - Only the local-wake path (`made_ready_here`) is exercised with them.
  - The receivers do exercise the other paths, but the delivery phase has no double-run detector.
  - Suggest one pulled integrity variant whose threads keep interrupts on outside the take and release of the word.
- **b. "At least one pull" is met in the first tick by pulling threads that have never run.** After the spread the source may take no tick at all:
  - `settled` never becomes true for threads moved back;
  - its `Rate` and `take_ask` never renew.
  
  So "both kinds of mover work on the same threads at once" is not shown. Report, and perhaps require, pulls of threads that had run and pulls after a move back.
- **c. Delivery's criterion is weaker than its comment says.** The comment reads "(as R96-2 asks of moves)". Phase 1 requires `carried_token`; the pulled phase accepts any pending wake-up, which may be the stale one the sender's second wake-up leaves. It can pass without a token's wake-up ever crossing a pull.
- **d. Nothing reads the counters the comments say a proof holds.** `Rate::read` and `timer::balance_sent`, `asks_taken()`, `COUNTS.too_soon`, `COUNTS.hot_across` and `longest_pass` are read by no proof and no line. The comments say otherwise:
  - "what a proof holds to 1";
  - "TASKS item 98's proof holds it there";
  - "the boot control C98-4 plants that".
  
  No C98 control exists in xtask on this branch. `asks_taken` has no caller, which under `-D warnings` is likely a `dead_code` error (not checked).
- **e. A vacuous pass.** With two or more processors and balancing not set up (`Masks::new` fails, or the domains are missing), `pulled_cannot_run` answers pass. The xtask markers match only the prefix `…, pulled: `, so the gate would not notice that the mechanism under proof never ran. Make "not set up" with two or more processors a failure, or add a marker for the "set up" line.

#### R98-4. The pulled integrity phase needs an idle processor in the start processor's cache
- **Severity:** risk (a false failure of a Refuse-class proof on a correct kernel). Condition: the plan gives the start processor (CPU 1, or the boot processor with two) no `shares_cache` level with an idle peer, for example one core per package with caches reported.
- **Where:** `migration_proof.rs` `pulled_start` (1408); `runqueue.rs` `quiet` (1240).
- **Scenario:** integrity threads switch every few microseconds and take no tick. `quiet` is never true on their processor, so none is ever cold and only a cache-sharing processor may take one. With none: "no pull in 30 s: scheduler failed".
- **Gate machines:** the `-smp` topologies in xtask (`sockets=1` or `2` with several cores) all give CPU 1 cache-mates, so the gate is not affected as far as I could read.
- **Change:** when no cache-sharing peer takes part, say so and pass; or start the threads where one exists.

#### R98-5. The pinned-mode line owed by a pull is printed only after the halt
- **Severity:** risk (a diagnostic delayed without bound).
- **Where:** `pull.rs` `idle` (270–287).
- **Scenario:** a pull meets `LendLost`. `pull_from` enters pinned mode and sets `owed`, `Pull::Stop` makes `pass` return false, and `idle` calls `cpu::idle_disabling()` inside the section. `pinned::line` runs only after the halt ends. If the interrupt that ends it switches to a thread, the line waits until the idle flow next runs. A panic in between loses the line that names the first trigger.
- **Change:** if `owed` is set, skip the halt this round: return, print, loop.

#### R98-6. `published()` can be true where `lend` answers `Leaving`; two comments say it cannot
- **Severity:** note. The code is safe: `TryLater` becomes `Pull::Nothing`.
- **Where:** `framekernel/frame/sync/src/contexts.rs` `published` (614), `publish_leaving` (592), `lend` (1222); `pull.rs` 507–509 ("neither can be its answer").
- **Scenario:** S releases its queue lock and runs `publish_leaving` in `resume`. P takes the lock and reads `published(F) == true` between S's store of the slot and its clear of the record. `lend` then reads `leaving == F` and refuses `Leaving`. This needs S stalled between two stores, which a descheduled virtual processor gives.
- **Change:** in `published`, read `leaving` first and answer false if it equals `frame`, the order `lend` uses. Then the comment is true and no move is begun in vain. Otherwise correct the comments and count the case.

#### R98-7. In-phase idle passes herd on one victim's queue lock
- **Severity:** risk at scale. Condition: tens of idle application processors and one crowded processor.
- **Where:** `framekernel/src/sched.rs` `run_ap` (every interrupt, each tick included, ends the halt and is followed by a pass); `pull.rs` `pull_from`; the test-and-set `SpinLock`.
- **Scenario:**
  - Every application processor starts its periodic timer right after `GO`, so their ticks coincide.
  - At each tick every idle one makes its pass, and all that see the same surplus queue on that victim's lock with interrupts off.
  - Each hold scans up to 15 slots times 32 table slots and writes the victim's element.
  - With N idle processors the victim's own tick or switch can wait about N holds for its lock, interrupts off: past B18's 50 µs from a few tens of processors, and worse under an emulated processor whose lock holder is descheduled.
- **Note:** liveness in fact rests on this re-pass at every tick, not on the kick. The module documentation should say so.
- **Change:** `try_lock` the victim and treat a failure as `Nothing`, or a per-victim "a puller is looking" flag; publish `longest_pass`.

#### R98-8. `lendable` ignores pins and the move rate, so a kick is spent on a thread nobody can take
- **Severity:** note.
- **Where:** `pull.rs` `kick` (675–680).
- **Scenario:** S holds an unsettled or pinned-by-count balanced thread beside two CPU-bound ones. Each tick's kick sends S's one interrupt for it. The ask's answer kick in the same tick is then `kicks_over_rate`, and the asker waits for its own next tick.
- **Change:** build `lendable` from the same tests a pull applies (pins, and `settled` for the narrowest period).

#### R98-9. Cost claims and small items
- **Severity:** note.
- **The "one load and one branch" claim is wrong** (`pull.rs`, module documentation). With no balanced thread:
  - the tick pays the out-of-line `ticked` (552): three per-CPU lookups, `SETUP.get()`, about eight loads, two of them on the shared hint words;
  - the reschedule upcall pays `left_idle`.
- **`made_ready_here` does everything before the rate is asked:** it takes the queue lock a second time, then `rdtsc`, four slot scans, `mfence` and the scan for an idle processor. A thread that makes balanced threads ready in a loop pays all of it each time for at most one interrupt a tick. Ask `timer::balance_spent` first, and then only publish. This matters once item 100 makes most threads balanced.
- **`kick` sends its IPI under the queue lock.** Harmless, but `wake` sends outside it.
- **`set_up` says "without the memory for the masks, nothing is set up".** Only the masks are fallible. `Plan::build`, `Box::new` and the two `collect`s abort on failure.
- **`Pull::Stop` for `destination.census().runnable != 0` halts with a thread known to be ready.** A spawn from another processor sends no interrupt, so it waits up to a tick, as before the change. Yielding there costs nothing.
- **`pull.rs` `idle` runs `sti; hlt` while the `InterruptsOff` token is in scope.** Nothing uses it after; it is a trap for a later edit.

#### R98-10. The "independent" counts share their clock or predicate with what they check
- **Severity:** note.
- **Where:** `balance.rs` `Rate::tick`/`note_sent` (334–353); `pull.rs` 477–483.
- `this_tick` is zeroed by the same `Rate::tick()` that clears `sent`. A second `balance_tick` call in one real tick would allow two interrupts and leave `most == 1`. Counting against the queue's tick number, as `note_ask` does, would be independent.
- `too_soon` and `hot_across` evaluate the same `settled` call and `cold` bit as the filter. They detect the filter's removal, not its being wrong.

#### R98-11. The randomized model cannot see two things it is cited for
- **Severity:** note.
- **Where:** `runqueue.rs` `balancing_against_a_model` (2625).
- Every queue is given the same `now`. A comparison of one processor's TSC with another's would pass. Give each queue its own offset.
- Every tick marks and judges. The kernel does neither on a tick with no balanced thread, so the stale-mark case is not run. I read it as sound (a stale mark is older, so "no switch since" is a stronger statement, and more arrivals count as fresh), but the model does not hold it.
- It is otherwise a real check of the one promise: `named_cold > 1000` keeps it from being vacuous, and the `fresh` rule was found by it.

### 2. What I checked and found sound

1. **No double run.** A candidate is balanced, ready and not current under the source's lock. The source's scheduler names a frame only under that lock, and `migrate` and other pulls need it. `published` then `lend` cannot lose the frame; `NotHere` is rightly S20's trigger 2. The FPU area is saved inside `with_queue` before the lock is released. `end_with` and `pin()` raise the pin with interrupts off while current, and a pull reads `pins()` under the lock.
2. **Locks.** `pull_from` takes both locks in index order after the `from == to` test, then the gate inside `carry`. `kick`, `tick_balanced`, `rescheduled_balanced` and `made_ready_here` hold their own queue's lock only, and `asks_taken` one at a time. No allocation, drop, print or shootdown runs under a lock; `moved_or_why` and `pinned::enter` run after `drop((low, high))`. `follow` releases each lock inside `locked`, so `made_ready_here` takes its lock with none held. A caller of `wake` holding this queue's lock would already have deadlocked in `follow`.
3. **Wake-ups.** `carry` is `migrate`'s own code. `depart` carries `wake_pending`; `arrive` marks woken. A wake-up's stale IPI to the source is harmless because the puller itself yields to the arrival.
4. **`publish_leaving_here`.** Its three callers are the tick upcall, the reschedule upcall and thread or handler context in `wake` or a spawn. All run in a later entry than the switch that wrote the record, before that entry's own `Contexts::switch`, and never on the leaving frame's stack. Interrupts are off from the pass to `sti; hlt`, so none is lost. An idle flow resumed late carries no decision across the halt.
5. **Neither misses the other.** Each side stores, then fences, then reads. The idle hint is set and cleared only by its owner. The crowded hint is set and cleared only under the owner's queue lock, so no set races a clear. A stale idle flag lasts at most a tick, and every idle application processor passes again at its own tick. So no missed kick halts a processor past one tick; R98-1 and R98-2 are the indefinite cases.
6. **Bounds.** `balance_spent`, `take_ask` and `send_balance_ipi` run in one interrupts-off stretch with no send between, so they are race-free. `take_ask` is under the source's lock. `arrive` stamps every move, so two pulls of one thread are separated by more than `period` delivered ticks of the queue it is on.
7. **Cache-hot.** `now` comes only from the queue's own processor in all three callers. `cold()` is void after any switch; `depart`, `arrive` and `push_weighted` clear the bit; `exit` always switches. Arrivals at or after the mark's tick are left out.
8. **Plan.** `shape.groups[0]` is guarded by `len() == 1`; every other access is `get`. An index outside the tree gets no levels. One processor gets none. Out-of-order trees are covered by the running maximum. A victim found at a wider level than the true one only makes the tests stricter.
9. **Pinned mode.** `pass`, `pull_from` (under the source's lock) and `kick` all test `withdrawn()`. The line is printed outside locks and outside the section (but see R98-5).
10. **Arithmetic.** Shifts are below 16 (`bit()`, `1 << slot` for slots 1 to 15, `was >= CAPACITY` guarded). Counters saturate or are atomics. TSC differences use `saturating_sub`. `scaled` is computed in u64.
11. **Stale load word.** A false surplus costs one look, and the looker republishes. A false "no surplus" is corrected by the owner's next tick-time kick while it holds a balanced thread. Nothing is decided from the word.
12. **Pulled phases, clean-up.** Each stops its threads, wakes them, waits for `ended == started`, drops `Shared` and clears `SHARED` before the compare. Late starters find `None` and finish. The drain in the delivery phase still fails on a lost pending wake-up.
13. **Switch path.** `with_queue`, `RunQueue::switch`, `follow_thread` and `on_yield` are textually unchanged. `wake` gains one load of `record.balanced` and a branch, only on a local wake that made the thread ready.
14. **Loom.** The three models call the real functions. BX1c and BX2 are caught for the stated reason even under sequential consistency. The `from` text of each control matches the source.
15. **Printing.** New lines carry counts, CPU indexes and static text. The new `Debug` impls write names only.

### 3. What I could not check without running

- **Whether Loom finds BX1a and BX1b.** This depends on how Loom 0.7.2 models `fence(SeqCst)` with a one-sided fence. `cargo xtask loom` settles it.
- **Lints.** `asks_taken` as dead code, and the unused `element` after BX2's edit, which should only warn. `cargo xtask check` settles it.
- **Whether "a pull carrying a pending wake-up" is reached within 30 s on every machine.** My reading:
  - with two and four processors, yes: receivers share the start processor, and stale pending wake-ups are common;
  - with 65, after the first spread, it depends on the boot flow catching a receiver between its wake-up and its processor's switch (the frame is published only if that processor idled across a tick), and on two receivers then sharing CPU 1 for more than `period` ticks. Likely, not certain.
  
  Several boots of the 2-, 4-, 65- and 67-processor machines, with the time printed in `reached_after`, would settle it.
- **R98-1 and R98-2 at boot.** A report-only phase on the two-package machine would show R98-1: two CPU-bound balanced threads on one processor of package 0, every other processor of package 0 busy with pinned threads, and a count of pulls expected above 0.
- **Static instruction counts** of `wake`, `on_tick` and `on_reschedule` against the base (ADR-0018 V1), and the measured `longest_pass` on the 65-processor machines (R98-7).

## 3. The second review: the working tree after section 2's answers (the reviewer's text)

I read the first review and the author's answers, then the current files: `framekernel/src/sched/pull.rs` (whole), the balancing parts of `framekernel/src/runqueue.rs` with its host test and randomized model, the changed parts of `framekernel/src/sched.rs`, `framekernel/src/migration_proof.rs` (module documentation, `Shared`/`InPulled`, `integrity_thread`, `receiver`, `sender`, and everything from `// ---- pulled`), `framekernel/frame/sync/src/balance.rs`, `contexts.rs` (`switch`, `publish_leaving`, `published`, `lend`, `adopt`), `framekernel/frame/src/timer.rs` and `cpu.rs` (the balancing functions, `idle`, `idle_disabling`, `without_interrupts`), `framekernel/src/balance.rs`, and the xtask markers. I ran nothing: no build, test or cargo, and no other system's source was opened.

**Verdict.** I found no way for two processors to run one context, no deadlock or lock-order fault, no lost wake-up and no way for `cold()` to name a thread that ran within the window. The two new bounds counts cannot pass 1 on a correct kernel. What remains is liveness and proof strength:
- the idle flow's list can still starve a victim (D98-1);

### Findings

#### D98-1. The fruitless list holds 8 victims; a ninth makes it cycle and starve the one that has a thread
- **Severity:** risk (liveness). Not reachable in item 98, where at most 12 threads are balanced, so at most 6 processors hold two. Reachable once TASKS item 100 makes most threads balanced, on the 65- and 67-processor machines.
- **Where:** `framekernel/src/sched/pull.rs`, `Idle::note` (342–347, a ring of `FRUITLESS = 8`), `pass` (431–450), and the module documentation, lines 27–29.
- **Scenario:** P is idle. Nine crowded processors V1 to V9 rank ahead of V10 in `choose` (nearer level or higher load), and each always gives `Nothing`. For example each holds two alternating balanced threads outside P's cache (D98-5's case), or a thread pinned by count. V10 has a cold, published thread.
1. Passes 1 to 3 note V1 to V8; the ninth note (V9) overwrites V1.
2. Next pass: V1 is free again and ranks ahead of V10, so it is looked at and noted, which frees V2, and so on.
3. Exactly one fruitless victim is always free and always ahead. `choose` never returns `None`, so `state.clear()` never runs and V10 is never looked at.
- **Smaller form:** `Pull::Asked` ends the pass and is not noted. A nearest victim that P asks at every pass, while a nearer idle processor takes the kick each time, keeps P from ever reaching a held victim that now has a thread.
- **Change:** make the remembered set able to hold every processor (a bitmask sized at set-up, one per idle flow), or keep a rotating start index instead of a list. Correct the documentation's "until every other crowded processor has had its turn", which holds only up to 8.

#### D98-2. The application processors' idle flow now runs with interrupts off everywhere but in the halt
- **Severity:** risk (an unstated change of behaviour, and a documented rule broken).
- **Where:** `framekernel/src/sched.rs` `run_ap` (1981–1987; its doc at 1954–1956 still says "idles with interrupts on"); `pull.rs` `idle` (371–399); `framekernel/frame/src/cpu.rs` `idle` (1303), `idle_disabling` (1311), `without_interrupts` (1977); `framekernel/frame/src/trap.rs` `section_off` (358–366).
- **What changed:**
- At the base the loop called `cpu::idle()`, which is `sti; hlt` with no `cli`. After the first halt the idle flow ran `reap_with` with interrupts on.
- Now the halt is `sti; hlt; cli` inside `without_interrupts_off`, which restores the flags it saved. An application processor enters `run_ap` with interrupts off, so the saved flag is always "off" and nothing turns them on again.
- So `reap_with(flow)` (zombie reaping, `stack::reclaim` and its TLB shootdown wait, a possible last drop of an address space) now runs with interrupts off on every application processor.
- **Scenario:** an application processor reaps a user thread whose address space it frees, or waits in a shootdown for a slow target (bounded by `WAIT_SECONDS = 1`). For that whole stretch it takes no tick and no reschedule interrupt. A thread woken on it waits, its queue's ticks stop, and a proof that watches its ticks sees a gap. At the base a tick preempted the reap.
- **Second point:** `section_off` and `without_interrupts_here` say their closure "must not enable interrupts, open an interrupt window, idle, yield…". `pull::idle` halts inside the section. The comment "nothing uses the token after the halt" is true today, but the rule is the framework's.
- **Change:** decide which state is meant and say it in `run_ap`'s doc.
- If interrupts are to stay off outside the halt, end the section after the pass and halt outside it. With interrupts already off that is the same single stretch, and the rule is kept.
- If the base's behaviour is wanted, turn interrupts on once before the loop; the section then restores "on" after each halt.
- **What would settle it:** one boot printing RFLAGS.IF at the top of `run_ap`'s loop on its second turn.

#### D98-3. "delivery, pulled" can pass on a kernel whose pull drops the pending wake-up
- **Severity:** risk (a proof that can pass with its property broken). The author's answer to R98-3 (c) leaves this open.
- **Where:** `framekernel/src/migration_proof.rs` `delivery_by_pulls` (1830–1832, 1890–1897); `receiver` (1150–1166); `pull.rs` 667.
- **Scenario:** a pull that drops `wake_pending` in `arrive` is planted.
- `pulls_carrying` is counted from `departed.wake_pending()` before the drop, so `carrying >= 1` still holds.
- The drain fails only if some receiver was pulled inside its spin with a token posted before the pull and then blocked on its old reading. Nothing requires that this happened in the run. With `in_window == 0` the phase passes.
- **`in_window` itself over-counts:** it is true when the receiver ended its spin off the start processor with a token posted during the spin. The token may have been posted after the pull, in which case the wake-up was left on the new processor and never crossed the pull. Making `in_window >= 1` a condition would still not show the carried path.
- **Change:**
- Until control CP-2 has been planted and seen to fail on every gate machine, the line and the module documentation should not say the drain guards a wake-up lost across a pull.
- For an exact count, have the receiver record the queue's count of its own pulls before the spin and after (or let the sender record, at its post, that the receiver was still on the start processor), and count the cases "posted before the pull, pulled, then blocked and returned".

#### D98-4. Neither pulled phase's "reached" condition is guaranteed by construction; both rest on the boot flow catching a thread in a short window
- **Severity:** risk (a false failure of a Refuse-class proof on a correct kernel). I could not decide it by reading.
- **Where:** `migration_proof.rs` `pull_back_for` (1653–1681), `integrity_by_pulls` (1736–1741, 1773), `delivery_by_pulls` (1826–1832, 1895), `PULLED_MOST` (1502).
- **Mechanism:**
- `Pulled::begin()` now runs after every thread has started. On a machine with more idle processors than threads (65 and 67: 63 or 65 idle, 12 threads), the threads are spread one to a processor by then, and the start processor holds one.
- No processor has a surplus after that, so no idle processor pulls anything until the boot flow has moved threads back.
- `migrate` moves only a thread that is ready, not running and published. A thread alone on its processor is in that state only between its wake-up and its processor's switch to it. For a receiver, its frame must also have been published by an interrupt taken while it was blocked.
- That window is long under an overcommitted host and a few microseconds on real cores.
- `again >= 1` then needs two returned threads to meet on the start processor, or one to wait there more than its period. `carrying >= 1` needs a returned receiver waiting there with a pending wake-up.
- **By machine, as I read it:**
- 2 and 4 processors: the start processor stays crowded for the whole run, so both conditions are reached early.
- 8: likely.
- 65 and 67: depends on the windows above within 30 s.
- **Other checks:** the queue of 16 slots is never over-filled (13 at most on the start processor, 12 on a puller). The 32-slot table of contexts is not over-filled either.
- **Change:** build the situation instead of waiting for it. For example, keep the start processor crowded by spawning one pinned spinner on every other processor that takes part but two or three, so that pulled threads double up and the boot flow always finds one waiting. Or require the condition only where `pulled.back >= 2` was reached, and report otherwise.
- **What would settle it:** several boots of the 2-, 4-, 8-, 65- and 67-processor machines under TCG and KVM, reading `reached_after` and the count of moves back.

#### D98-5. R98-1's own case is not fixed: two equal threads that alternate at every tick are never taken across caches
- **Severity:** defect against ADR-0020 S7's work-conserving claim, as in the first review. The author states it as a limit awaiting a ruling. No safety effect.
- **Where:** `pull.rs` `tick_balanced` (734–745), `kick` (845–863), module documentation 60–65; `runqueue.rs` `judge` (1283).
- **Scenario:** S runs T1 and T2, equal weight, never yielding. P is idle outside S's last-level cache.
1. At each tick `judge` names the waiter cold, and the step then runs it.
2. The thread just switched out is `out`, so `lendable == 0` and no kick is sent.
3. P's pass counts `hot` and returns before the published check, so it sends no ask.
- **Wider than the documentation says:** the same holds with one balanced and one pinned thread of equal weight, and on the boot processor with the boot flow and one balanced thread. The limit is "exactly two runnable contexts of equal stride", not "two balanced threads".
- **Wording:** "nothing looks then" should read "nothing judges then". Only S judges, and only at its own upcalls.
- **What the answer does fix:** the wider form (a running thread that blocks often) and three or more contexts. I checked both in the code.
- **Change:** a ruling. If the limit stays, amend S7's sentence in the ADR. Otherwise let S judge once more from a one-shot timer or at the first upcall at least H after its mark.

#### D98-6. Answer R98-7 says the diagnostic round prints `contended`; nothing reads it

#### D98-7. The cost section leaves out what `block`, `sleep` and others now pay; "none of it on the path of a switch" is not exact

- **Where:** `pull.rs` 84–93; `runqueue.rs` module documentation 59–60; `sleep` (973), `block_for` (1000), `preempt_for_wake` (929), `push_weighted` (693–704), `exit` (1046–1049).

#### D98-8. `kick` counts a thread lendable with a period of 1, whatever the machine's narrowest period is

### Checked and found sound

1. **No double run.** A candidate is balanced, ready and not current under the source's lock. `published` true means a slot holds the frame, and only the source's own switch (which needs that lock to name the thread), another mover (same lock) or a withdrawal of a never-queued thread can take it. `lend` cannot answer `NotHere` for it, so no false entry to pinned mode. `carry`'s two panics are unreachable after `frame(slot)` and `has_room()` under the same locks.
2. **Locks.** `take` is a bounded `try_lock` and builds a guard only on success. Both orders are by index. Every early return drops what it holds. `drop(source_guard)` comes after the last use of `source`. The ask is one register write under the puller's own lock, and the source's upcall takes only its own lock. `kick`, `made_ready_here` and `bounds()` hold one lock at a time. `wake` holds no queue lock when it calls `made_ready_here`. Pinned mode is entered and its line printed after both guards are dropped.
3. **`pass` terminates.** Every turn either adds a look (at most `TRIES`) or clears a non-empty list, which needs a note since the last clear. That is at most 2×`TRIES`+1 turns.
4. **No missed interrupt before the halt.** The pass and `sti; hlt` are one interrupts-off stretch. A wake-up's or a kick's interrupt that arrives meanwhile ends the halt at once. `Passed::Run` yields. The pinned-mode line skips the halt once, and the next pass halts because moves are withdrawn (R98-5 answered).
5. **Wake-ups across a pull.** `carry` is `migrate`'s own code: `depart` carries the pending wake-up, `arrive` restores it and marks the thread woken, and the location follows under both locks and the gate.
6. **Cache-hot rule.**
 - `mark`'s TSC is read after the tick's interrupt and before the step, so a thread in `mark_waiting` has not run since that reading.
 - Every way off the processor shows on the thread: a tick or a yield charges at least 1024 to its pass (a pass at `u64::MAX` is excluded), and a block, a sleep or a preemption sets `ran`.
 - `depart` clears the bits. `push_weighted` resets them for a slot's next thread. `arrive` clears `mark_waiting`.
 - A stale mark only matters while no balanced thread is on the queue, and then nothing is named.
 - `judge`, `mark` and the TSC are the queue's own processor's in all three callers. A puller reads `cold()` without a TSC.
7. **Bounds counts.** `note_sent` runs under the sender's own queue lock, against `RunQueue::ticks`, apart from `Rate`. The queue's tick and the rate's renewal happen in the same upcall, in that order, before any send. `note_ask` and `take_ask` run under the source's lock. `too_soon` and `hot_across` repeat the filter's own tests. None can pass its limit on a correct kernel.
8. **`integrity_thread` with interrupts on.** The word is taken and released by one sequential execution with the value kept in a local, so a preemption or a move between take and release cannot fail a swap. The stale index in `on` is only a non-zero tag. XMM canaries are saved under the queue lock before a puller can take the thread, and restored from the record's area on the new processor. The pair protocol does not deadlock with preemption. A real double run still fails a take or a release, short of an unlucky interleaving, as in the first phase.
9. **Answers that hold as written:** R98-3 (a), (b), (d) apart from D98-6, (e); R98-5; R98-6 (code); R98-7's bounded tries; R98-9's `made_ready_here`; R98-10. `Rate::read`, `timer::balance_sent` and `asks_taken` are gone with no reference left.
10. **Printing and arithmetic.** The new lines carry counts, CPU indexes and static text. Shifts are below 16 (`was >= CAPACITY` is guarded; slots run 1 to 15). Counters saturate. TSC differences use `saturating_sub`.
11. **Compile reading.** I saw no error in the new code: the borrows in `pull_from` and `pass`, the let-chains, `CpuIndex`'s `Display` and `SpinGuard`'s path all check out by reading.
