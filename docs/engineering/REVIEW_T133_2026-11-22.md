## Findings

### R133-1 (Medium): the rental tool reads only the success line as "returned". A call that returned with errors reads as a hang, and a lost success line reads as reset (S) or "other"

**Where:**
- `developer-tools/bench/latitude.py:1097` (`RETURNED_LINE`);
- `developer-tools/bench/latitude.py:1316` (`returned = called and RETURNED_LINE in text`);
- `developer-tools/bench/latitude.py:1359-1363`.

The lines the kernel-entry loader writes after the call come from three places:
- `boot/uefi/src/main.rs:869-887`, after a successful call;
- `boot/uefi/src/main.rs:909-921`, after two failed calls;
- `boot/uefi/src/main.rs:1486-1488` and `1855-1871`.

**What is wrong.** Ruling Q5 asks the tool to read "a line after X" as the call returning (R). The
kernel-entry loader writes three kinds of line straight to the UART, and only after a call has
returned:
1. `bench::Exited`, "origin-boot: bench build: boot services exited …", after `EFI_SUCCESS`;
2. Amendment 2's line, "origin-boot: ExitBootServices failed (…): halting", after two failed calls;
3. TASKS item 116's `AfterCall` line, "origin-boot: bench build: after ExitBootServices (…", after
   either outcome.

The tool counts only the first as R. `bench_exited` writes the `Exited` line to the UART as the
firmware's exit left it, before `bench_after_call` writes the firmware's values back. It is the line
R116-2 says "may not be readable". The `AfterCall` line, written after the write-back, was put there
to be the first readable one.

**Failure scenario.** Reproduced with `review133/py/probe.py` on the tool at `d12f6ecf`. Each case is
a console with the kernel-entry loader's lines, then X:
- **A hang that is not one.** Y, the halting line and the `AfterCall` line follow, then nothing,
  because both calls failed and the loader halted. The tool reads `answer=none`. `describe` gives "a
  call, XY, then nothing for 349.9 s: no reset within 300.9 s of its line", and the summary "of the 1
  judged, a reset after 0, nothing after 1", the hang bucket. Rental C ranked a failed retry as the
  first cause, so this outcome is realistic for rental I. The record would count it as reading A.
- **A reset (S) that is not one.** The `Exited` line is lost or garbled, the `AfterCall` line arrives
  whole, then the machine starts again 5.9 s later. That restart could come, for example, from the
  entry stub faulting on its CR3 load, before its `R`. The tool reads `answer=reset`. `describe` gives
  "then the firmware again 5.9 s after its line". That is reading S, "the firmware's exit shutting
  down", although the call returned and Origin's own code then reset the machine.
- **"Other" output.** The same, but the breadcrumbs and kernel lines follow. The tool reads
  `answer=other`, with the text "RS12345678 origin-kernel: console: …", not R.

**Fix.**
1. In `read_runs`, take as R any of the three lines that appears in the run's text after its first
   mark. The halting line and the `AfterCall` prefix are written only after a call. Keep two answers
   apart, for example `returned` (`EFI_SUCCESS`) and `returned-error` (Amendment 2's line), so that
   `describe` and `runs_summary` say which.
2. Add the three cases above to `test_a_kernel_entry_images_calls_read_returned_reset_or_hang`.

### R133-2 (Low): another image's reading changes. The full bench image's calls that return now read as "returned", and their reset is no longer reported

**Where:** `developer-tools/bench/latitude.py:1316` and `1359-1363` (no image-kind test);
`developer-tools/bench/latitude.py:1462-1470`.

**What is wrong.** `returned` is computed for every image. TASKS item 116's full bench loader (no
`BENCH-AFTER`) also writes `bench::Exited` after a successful call, then its reset lines. Such a run
now stops at "returned", so the firmware's start after `ResetSystem` is no longer read: no `after_s`,
and no count in "a reset after N". The brief asks whether any other image's reading changes. This one
does.

**Failure scenario.** Reproduced with `probe.py`:
- console: the full bench loader's lines, X, `Exited`, `AfterCall`, "a cold reset through the
  firmware's ResetSystem in 5 s …", then the firmware 5.9 s later;
- `f1a4d882`'s tool reads "the firmware again 5.9 s after its line", with "a reset after 1, 5.9 s
  after the mark";
- `d12f6ecf`'s tool reads "returned … no kernel line followed", with "a reset after 0".

Whether Amendment 3's `ResetSystem` reset the board is what that image is rented to show, and it is
now missing from the boot's summary. Re-reading rentals B to F is unaffected: none of them printed
`Exited`.

**Fix.** One of two:
- compute `returned` only for kernel-entry runs, with `KERNEL_BUILD_LINE` in the run's text;
- or keep R and still read the next firmware start into `after_s`, so that `describe` says both.

Add a test with the full bench image's returning run.

### R133-3 (Low): after two failed calls, the kernel-entry mode reads the UART's registers, writes them back and writes the `AfterCall` line before the halt. Q5 and the module's text say Amendment 2's line, then the halt

**Where:**
- `boot/uefi/src/main.rs:909-921`;
- `boot/uefi/src/main.rs:1855-1876` (`bench_after_call`, kept in this mode, with only its reset
  gated off);
- `boot/uefi/src/main.rs:2897` (the module documentation: "after two failed calls, Amendment 2's
  line and the halt").

**What is wrong.** The halt path is the full bench mode's path with `BENCH-AFTER kernel`:
1. `say_halting`;
2. `bench_after_call`: the 16550's registers read, the firmware's divisor, LCR, IER, MCR and FCR
   written back, and the `AfterCall` line;
3. `halt()`.

Q5 says "after two failed calls: Amendment 2's line, then the halt", and Amendment 2 says the loader
"does not reprogram" the UART. `check_kernel_halt` (`developer-tools/xtask/src/bench.rs:2124`, through
`check_halt_in`, which requires exactly `[X, Y, HALTING, AFTER_BOTH_FAILED]`) writes the extra line
into the expected result.

**Failure scenario.** On a rented board where both calls fail, the console shows the halting line,
then `origin-boot: bench build: after ExitBootServices (first call …, retry …) the UART read …; the
firmware's values are written back`, then the halt. That is a UART reprogramming after a failed

**Fix.** Keeping it is probably right, since R133-1's fix wants that line. Then:
- correct the `kernel_entry` documentation;
- have the coordinating session record that ruling Q5's halt path includes TASKS item 116's line

Otherwise, leave out `bench_after_call` in this mode's `Halt` arm and change `check_kernel_halt` to
three lines.

### R133-4 (Low): every kernel-entry boot prints "after ExitBootServices the loader resets the machine, and the kernel is not entered"

**Where:**
- `boot/uefi/src/main.rs:222` (`bench::After::said(found)`, still printed in this mode);
- `boot/uefi/src/main.rs:229-234`;
- the scenarios that expect it: `developer-tools/xtask/src/bench.rs:2085`, `2091`, `2111` and `2125`
  (`NO_AFTER`).

**What is wrong.** With no `BENCH-AFTER`, which is the rented case, the console's first line about the
switch says the loader resets the machine and does not enter the kernel. For this image both claims
are false. The next line, `KERNEL_AFTER_IGNORED`, contradicts it. That pair also contains
"BENCH-AFTER", the tool's marker for a resetting image. This is why `resets_after_call` needs its new
special case at `latitude.py:1514`.

**Failure scenario.** A reader of rental I's `sol.log`, or of a record quoting it, sees "the loader
resets the machine, and the kernel is not entered" on every run of the image that is meant to enter
the kernel.

**Fix.** In this mode, print a line of the mode's own in place of `After::said(found)`. For example:
"bench build: kernel: BENCH-AFTER <absent | value>: read and ignored; the kernel is entered after a
successful call". Then update `check_kernel_console`'s `after_line` arguments.

### R133-5 (Low): Q4's check has no positive control inside any gate, matches one instruction shape only, and does not run on the stack-measurement loaders

**Where:**
- `developer-tools/xtask/src/bench.rs:526-538` (`os_type_allocations`);
- `developer-tools/xtask/src/bench.rs:543-558`;
- `developer-tools/xtask/src/bench.rs:3149` (the `#[ignore]` test);
- the call sites: `developer-tools/xtask/src/reproducible.rs:259` and
  `developer-tools/xtask/src/bench.rs:388`, `438`, `484`, `926`.

**What is wrong.** The ruling asks for the pre-change loader as the check's positive control.
- **The control.** Inside the gates there is only `OLD_ALLOCATIONS`, a text fixture, which shows that
  the matcher matches the text it was written from. The real-image control is an ignored test that
  nothing runs. I ran it by hand on the old full bench and release loaders, and it passes today.
- **The matcher.** It finds only `mov edx|rdx, 0x80000002|0x80000003`. If a change brought a type
  back, it could be compiled into another shape and pass vacuously, with no in-gate control to show
  it. Examples:
  - the constant kept in a callee-saved register and copied (`mov ebx, 0x80000003` … `mov edx, ebx`);
  - one constant derived from the other, since they differ by one (`lea edx, [rbx + 1]`);
  - the constant loaded from memory.
- **Coverage.** The stack-measurement loaders (`stack_depth::build_loader`, TASKS item 127) are
  loaders too, and are not checked.

**Failure scenario.** Suppose a later change gives the boot info and the stack their old types
through a two-entry table, `[OS_KERNEL_STACK, OS_BOOT_INFO][i]`, or through a helper that LLVM
specialises with a register copy. The reproducible gate and every bench build then pass, and the
ignored test is not run.

**Fix.**
1. Widen the matcher: any instruction whose operand is the immediate `0x80000002` or `0x80000003`,
   matched as a whole word so that 64-bit EFI status values do not match.
   - At `d12f6ecf` no loader should hold either constant at all, because `kind_of` no longer
     compares them. The old images' listings show the two moves and the two compares as their only
     uses.
   - A false positive would need new code using CPUID leaves `0x80000002`/`0x80000003`, which the
     loader does not read.
2. Record the ignored test's run on a kept pre-change image in the task's turn.
3. Call `check_no_os_types` from `stack_depth::build_loader` too.

## Checked and found sound

1. **The amendment's claim that the kernel is unchanged.** No file under `framekernel/` or `boot/info/`
   changed. The kernel and the paging library use the boot info and the region kinds in four places:
   - `layout::direct_mapped`, which maps `BootInfo`, `KernelStack` and `LoaderReclaimable` alike;
   - `layout::build`'s guard rule, `KernelStack` with `base == stack_guard`;
   - `origin_boot_info::boot_seed`, which wants the area inside a `BootInfo` region;
   - `early.rs`'s `stack_guard = stack_base - PAGE` and its probe of that page.

   `frames.rs` and `memory.rs` free and reclaim only `Usable` and `LoaderReclaimable`, so the carved
   `KernelStack` and `BootInfo` are never reclaimed. Nothing in the workspace outside the loader's
   tests still names `0x8000_0002`/`0x8000_0003`. `protocols::memory_type::OS_*` are left in place
   for the tests.

   The loader translates four maps:
   - **The SPCR and FCH walk's map:** used only by `spcr::described` and `fch::map_says`. Every kind
     but MMIO and unaccepted memory counts as described, so `LoaderReclaimable` in place of
     `KernelStack` changes nothing.
   - **The table-sizing map:** both kinds are direct-mapped. The bound's region ends come from
     `capacity`, and later carving cannot exceed it.
   - **The page-table map** and **the exit map:** both are carved.

   For a given physical layout, carving gives exactly the regions the old OS-defined types gave.
   Translation now merges the stack's and the boot info's pages into their neighbouring loader data,
   and each carve splits that region at the same boundaries the old types made. The final count and
   the kinds are therefore the same, and no region array that fit before overflows now.
2. **Q2.** `kind_of` sends both old types to `FirmwareOther`. Two host tests hold such descriptors
   (`a_firmware_region_of_the_old_boot_info_type_gives_no_secret_area`, and the `NotLoaderData` case
   for the boot info), and the first has a positive control. Reverting either type's mapping fails
   `every_uefi_type_has_a_kind` and the `boot_seed` assertion.
3. **`carve_within` and `carve_loader_regions`.**
   - **Ordering:** the kernel, then the stack, then the boot info; the three are disjoint, so the
     order does not matter.
   - **Overlap:** a range across two regions is `NotContained`.
   - **Capacity:** checked before anything is written. A failure part-way leaves a partly carved
     array, but every caller then stops.
   - **A merged region holding several allocations:** tested. The stack and the boot info are
     adjacent in one block with loader data after them.
   - **The retry:** it translates a fresh map before it carves, so nothing is carved twice.
   - **The guard:** named from `stack - PAGE`, with 17 pages, as the old type's allocation was.
   - No arithmetic underflows (`base - r.base` only after `r.base <= base`), and nothing in it calls
     the firmware.
4. **`main.rs`'s two call sites and errors.** At the page-table site, `MapFailure::LoaderRegion`
   reports on the console with boot services intact. In the exit closure only the pure carve runs,
   and the report is made after the closure: `NotAttempted` before the first call. After a failed
   first call it becomes `Halt`, with Amendment 2's line, as `KernelRegion` did before.
   - A pre-existing caveat: a `NotAttempted` failure in the exit closure comes after "exiting boot
     services", which `latitude.py` counts as a call (R130-3).
   - That is practically unreachable here, since the page-table carve of the same allocations
     already passed.
5. **What each flavour compiles.** Everything the new `cfg`s gate is still compiled in the normal,
   full bench, minimal, bisection and stack-measurement flavours. Each changes only by Amendment 7's
   types, the carving and `kind_of`. In the bisection mode the page-table map is now carved too, so
   its tables leave the guard page unmapped. That follows from the amendment, and the mode never
   loads them.

   Clippy is clean in all seven flavours. The `compile_error!` forbids the kernel-entry mode with
   another mode.
6. **The kernel-entry mode against Q5.** The following are compiled out:
   - `bench_fch_decide`, `reset_without_firmware` and `write_cf9`;
   - `bench_reset`, `write_reset_register` and `wait_ms`;
   - `triple_fault` and `wait_ticks`.

   With them out:
   - no path calls `ResetSystem`;
   - none writes the FADT's register, which is I/O port `0xCF9` on the rented board;
   - none writes `0xCF9` directly.

   `check_kernel_code` checks the last of these in machine code, with the full bench loader as its
   control. The FCH is still read and printed, and `plan.free` is always `None`. After `EFI_SUCCESS`
   the mode takes TASKS item 112's path into `enter_kernel_bench`. The kernel's own code holds no
   write to `0xCF9` and no machine reset (searched). The AML interpreter running the firmware's
   methods was not reviewed.

   The three switch files:
   - **`BENCH-AFTER`** is read, printed and ignored (but see R133-4).
   - **`BENCH-FCH`** only supplies values for the printed lines.
   - **`BENCH-STALE-KEY`** is still honoured, as in the bisection mode. On a rented board it cannot be
     present, because the tool's install refuses an ESP holding any of the three
     (`latitude.py:95-100`).
7. **`xtask`.**
   - **The flag's lists and F1:** `git grep` finds the flag in exactly the four `KERNEL_FILES`. Each
     file is on `FILES`, and the F1 test covers an unlisted file.
   - **Signing:** `is_bench` includes `target/bench-kernel`, so signing refuses the image.
   - **The flavour's build settings:** consistent with `Loader::BenchKernel`, `build_kernel_loader`,
     the toolchain entries and its test, and the Clippy step.
   - **`build_kernel`'s text checks:** none of the images in `target/` made the marker by placing
     two strings side by side.
   - **`check_no_os_types` in the reproducible gate:** reads the first build's kept copy of each of
     the three normal loaders.
   - **`os_type_allocations` on real images:** it finds the old loaders' two moves and not their
     compares. Its fixture test catches dropping `rdx`, a type, or the `mov` filter.
   - **The five scenarios:** they would catch a reset after a successful call (no breadcrumbs, or
     QEMU exiting), and a reset or kernel entry after two failed calls (a fifth line, QEMU exiting,
     or kernel lines). They would also catch `BENCH-AFTER fadt` or `BENCH-FCH first` being obeyed
     (no `X`, or no breadcrumbs), and missing pieces of the full bench work (the console's line
     list).
8. **The rental tool.**
   - **The image kind:** `resets_after_call` is false for a kernel-entry console (mutant-checked by
     the new test), `loader_kind` names the image, and `breadcrumbs` reads it.
   - **No false R from `bench::Exited`:** it is the only source of `RETURNED_LINE`'s text, written
     only after `ExitOutcome::Exited`. The run's window is bounded by the next banner.
   - **S and silence:** silence after X reads as a hang, and a restart after X with nothing between
     reads as S, as Q5 rules. Inherent: under that reading, a call that returned to a dead UART and
     then a stub or kernel triple fault also reads as S.
