## What holds

This comes first, because apart from T78-R1 the findings are about evidence, robustness and records, not about the rule's soundness.

- **The fence (Q7) does what it claims, by both vendors' text.**
  - **Intel.** RDTSCP waits until every earlier instruction has executed. An LFENCE straight after it keeps every later instruction from starting, even speculatively, until it completes. RDTSC needs LFENCE before it to wait for earlier instructions, and LFENCE after it to hold later ones back.
  - **AMD.** RDTSCP makes every older instruction retire before it reads the counter. The white paper calls it dispatch serializing, so the RDMSR after it cannot execute first, whatever MSR C001_1029 holds.
  - **So**, with RDTSCP, `before()` is read before the count's RDMSR starts, and `after()` is read once the RDMSR has executed, on both makers. The LFENCE-only form is ordered wherever LFENCE is a barrier, as the ledger says.
  - **The compiler** keeps the blocks' order, since `asm!` without `pure` is volatile.
  - **Flags.** `after()`'s RDTSCP-only block may declare `preserves_flags`: neither manual lists a flag RDTSCP affects, and it is on `FLAG_FREE`. The blocks with LFENCE do not declare it. That is conservative: AMD lists no flag for LFENCE.
- **The period's bounds are sound.** Take each count as the whole count at some instant inside its read's bracket, and the count as linear. Then:
  - the count ticks between two samples are within one of `rate × time`;
  - that time lies between `last.before − first.after` and `last.after − first.before`;
  - `period_low` rounds down over `fall + 1`, and `period_high` rounds up over `fall − 1`.

  So `[period_low, period_high]` holds the true period of any pair that hides no reload. The middle-based `period` that the lines print lies inside it.
- **The stretch rule cannot be fooled below 20 times fast, for any span and any number of hidden reloads.** This is stronger than the argument written in the code (T78-R6). Let `obs` be a pair's fall and `N` the initial count.
  1. A pair that hides `k ≥ 1` reloads has `obs + k·N − 1 < rate·Δ`. Since `obs ≤ N`, that gives `rate·Δ > 2·obs − 1`.
  2. `judge_pair` reaches a verdict only if `high ≤ 2·pair_low`, where `pair_low = (obs − 1)/longest ≤ (obs − 1)/Δ`.
  3. Every stretch's upper bound exceeds the true rate. No step of at most a twentieth of the period can hide a reload while the period is longer than that, up to one count tick: a factor of 20·(1 − 1/N).
  4. So a verdict would need `2·obs − 1 < 2·obs − 2`, which is impossible. The `−1` and `+1` margins in `Slope::between` are exactly what make it strict.

  A slow timer gains nothing from a hidden reload: it reads slower still. So I1, I2 (within its linear model) and I3 (below 20×) hold.
- **Host evidence of the same** (with T78-R1 fixed locally):
  - **R-A, a broad adversarial search:** 60,000 cases. Timers from 19.99× fast to 2.5× slow; brackets up to 200 µs; counts sampled at the bracket's start, its end, its middle, a random point, or alternately; held reads up to 2 periods; gaps up to and just over P/100. 39,842 decided and 10,146 were in bounds. None was wrong.
  - **R-B, a targeted search:** 2,308,176 cases, from 1.9× to 19.99× in steps of 0.01×, with 1 to 20 hidden reloads. Each held read is timed so that the pair reads 97.05 % to 102.95 % of nominal (`d = k·T/(u·f − 1)`), with 0, 2 or 20 reads in a stretch before it, three read spacings, and samples at either end of their brackets. None was in bounds. On a subsample, the Python port with the stretch checks removed (C2 alone) passed 7,933 of 18,792, so the search has teeth.
  - **R-C, near the bounds:** 30,000 cases from 96 % to 104 %, with stalls and samples at the brackets' ends. None was wrong.
  - **R-D, above 20 times fast:** wide reads alternating with zero-width reads first pass at 20.94× fast, reading 1,029,028 ppm. "Below 20" is the true edge, with about 5 % to spare in this construction.
- **I4 to I7, the rest of the invariants.**
  - **I4:** undecided maps to `Stalled` and never to `InBounds`; there are still 4 runs; a run ends at 64 fifths of the period of TSC time (T78-R8 is about the read cap); every run prints its figures line, and every repeat its own line.
  - **I5:** `tick_verdict` is unchanged.
  - **I6:** the widest figures line is 158 bytes, which a test pins. The "measured again" line stays under 160 bytes even with `tries` at `u32::MAX` (149 bytes at 5-digit milliseconds), though `tries` is no longer bounded by 64. No new or changed line holds a `boot_failures()` marker, a failure word or `0x`.
  - **I7:** the only `unsafe` added is the fenced readings in `cpu.rs`. The sampler is still a pure function over a read closure.
- **Liveness, R-F:** a correct timer, with every bracket 30, 60, 120, 200 or 240 µs and the count sampled at the start, the end or alternately, decided in bounds every time, within 2 pairs.

## Findings

### T78-R1 (High): the tip's host tests do not compile, so `check` fails and none of the claimed host evidence runs at `05165dec`

- **Where:** `framekernel/frame/src/apic.rs:1528` and `:1531`, in the test helper `stalled_reads`, new in `05165dec`. The same lines are in `b819800c` and `f0c0cce5`.
- **What:** `let mut n = 0;` is never given a type before `n.is_multiple_of(every)`. A method call on an `{integer}` whose type is not yet known is error E0689.
  - So `cargo test -p origin-frame` and `cargo clippy -p origin-frame --all-targets` both fail to compile the crate's tests. `cargo xtask check` runs `cargo test --package origin-frame` (`xtask/src/main.rs:5420`), so `check` fails at the tip.
  - TASKS item 78 says the 28 `apic::` tests passed, and that the host Clippy's one finding, `is_multiple_of` in a test, was fixed before the commit. That fix is this line, so the tests were not compiled after it.
- **Failing input:** `cargo test --locked -p origin-frame --lib apic::` on an unmodified export of `05165dec`: `error[E0689]: can't call method 'is_multiple_of' on ambiguous numeric type '{integer}'` at `apic.rs:1531:30`, exit 101.
- **Fix:** `let mut n = 0u64;`. With only that changed, all 28 existing `apic::` tests pass, in debug and in release. After any Clippy-driven edit to test code, run `cargo test` again before the commit, not only Clippy on the kernel's target.

### T78-R2 (Medium): T78-1 and T78-1b would be refused on the tip even without the stretch rule, and T78-1's base half has a 7 µs window

- **Where:** the planned controls; `controls.py` (`hide=(2, 2, 9)` and `hide=(1, 5, 13)`); `apic.rs:456` (a gap resets the pair) and `:529` (set aside).
- **What, on the tip.** The tip refuses the plant by one of two paths:
  - **(a)** the held pair is undecided at read 3, because its stretches are single reads. It is set aside at read 4 by the stretch rule, and a later pair decides `Fast`.
  - **(b)** a gap over P/100, after the held read or right after read 3, breaks the held pair before the stretch rule sees it, and a later pair decides `Fast`.

  Path (b) is what broke the held pair in two of the three (p2) boots (ledger §3), and C2 alone also refuses by path (b). So "refused at once on the count" would hold for a tip that lacked C4 altogether. R-G shows both paths: with no gap after read 3 the tip decides `Fast` with `aside 1, gap 0`; with a gap there it decides `Fast` with `aside 0, gap 1`, for both plants.
- **What, on the base.** On `fcef041d` the plant passes only if the held pair's period lands in 97 % to 103 %. That means read 3's RDMSR must sample the count soon after the hold loop's last poll. R-H, run on the base's own `sample_period` (in Rust, and the same in the port):
  - T78-1 passes with read 3 up to 6 µs late, and is `Fast` from 8 µs late.
  - T78-1b passes up to 45 µs late, and is `Fast` at 60 µs.

  Between the poll and read 3 lie the poll's granularity, `fence.after()`, the sampler's loop body, and read 3's branch running for the first time. Under TCG, first-run code costs translation; the ledger saw the diagnostic's held pair broken that way in every boot. So T78-1's base run is likely to be `Fast` or broken by a gap, and then it shows nothing.
- **Fix:**
  - For T78-1 and T78-1b on the tip, accept a run only if its figures line shows `1 aside`, its failure line shows a period near 100,000 or 277,778 ppm, and no gap broke the held pair.
  - For the base half, widen the window. Hide more reloads at the same factor: at 10× with `hide=(8, 8, 9)`, read 3 may be about 30 µs late (the window is about 860 to 919 µs, around 889 µs). Or aim the hold at the window's low edge: at 10× with two reloads, about 0.215 of the period rather than 2/9. Or record the base boot as informative only, and let the host test stand as the base comparison.
  - Add `0 aside` to every correct-timer control: T78-2a to 2c, T78-5, T78-6, and T66-13 and T66-30 re-run. Under the model a correct timer is never set aside (every interval holds the true rate), so an `aside` there is itself a finding: the count is not linear, or a bracket does not enclose its read.

### T78-R3 (Medium): T78-3 is refused by delivery alone, so it does not show the count deciding with 120 µs reads

- **Where:** the plan's T78-3 (`dict(fast=200, wide=120)`), and `tick_verdict` (`apic.rs:798`).
- **What:** at twice the rate, the 50 interrupts come in about 250 ms. `judge_ticks` gives `TooFast` (under 95 % of 500 ms), and `tick_verdict(TooFast, Undecided)` is `TooFast` (R-J). So T78-3 is refused at once whatever the count does: an undecided count with 120 µs brackets gives the same result as a decided one. The same holds for T66-28, whose ADR-0013 row says "at once" but, rightly, not "on the count".
- **Fix:** add T66-22b's hold (`hold=(10, 300, "every")`) so that delivery looks right. Then require the failure line's `period` near 500,000 ppm and a figures line with `best` under ±30,000 ppm.

### T78-R4 (Low): T78-4's expected path sits on two edges

- **Where:** the plan's T78-4 (`dict(fast=105, wide=200)`).
- **What:** at 1.05× fast, two thresholds sit close to the plant.
  - **Delivery:** 50 interrupts take 476.2 ms, against the `TooFast` bound of 475 ms, a margin of 0.25 %.
  - **The count:** the upper bound of a 9 ms pair with 200 µs brackets sampled at their start is about 97.3 %, 0.3 % from 97 %.

  The calibration's error under TCG was −0.45 % to +0.55 % (ADR-0013). R-I:

  | Calibration error | Result |
  |---|---|
  | −0.5 % | The count decides `Fast` in run 1. |
  | −0.3 % | Delivery is `TooFast` in run 1. |
  | −0.1 % or more | Undecided, measured again, as planned. |

  The boot is refused in every case, so I3 is not at stake. But "undecided in every run, refused at run 4" may not be what the record shows.
- **Fix:** use 1.04×. Delivery is then 480.8 ms, and the count's bounds are about 94.1 % to 98.3 %, which still straddle 97 %. Or state the expectation as "never passes: refused at run 1 (fast, on the count or the delivery) or at run 4 (undecided)", and record which happened.

### T78-R5 (Low): anyone can build `TscFence::Rdtscp`, so the safe `before()` and `after()` can execute RDTSCP where there is none

- **Where:** `framekernel/frame/src/cpu.rs:1824-1835`: `pub enum TscFence { Rdtscp, Lfence }` with public variants and `Copy`. The SAFETY comment at `:1852` relies on "`Rdtscp` comes only from `detect`".
- **What:** the type does not enforce that invariant. Code in the kernel crate, which is `forbid(unsafe_code)` (`framekernel/src/lib.rs:110`) and relies on the framework's safe API, can write `cpu::TscFence::Rdtscp.after()`. On a processor or guest without RDTSCP, that is #UD.
  - It is not memory unsafety: the kernel's #UD handler stops the boot.
  - It is a safe function in the one privileged crate whose documented safety invariant the type does not carry, which is what §13 and ADR-0011 N6's split are meant to rule out.
- **Fix:** make the choice opaque, so that only `detect()` can produce the RDTSCP form. One way: `pub struct TscFence(Kind)` with a private `enum Kind`, built only by `detect()`, plus a `pub const LFENCE` for `ordered_tsc(false)`. Behaviour is unchanged.

### T78-R6 (Low): the written safety argument relies on a 0.9-period cap that the code does not have, and the band near twice the rate has little test evidence

- **Where:** `apic.rs:418-420`: "Under about 2.06 times fast, such a pair cannot fit in 0.9 of a period." ADR-0013's stated limit repeats it.
- **What:**
  - **Nothing caps a pair's span.** `LONGEST_PAIR_TENTHS` only stops an undecided pair from going on. A single step can carry a pair from under 0.9 of the period to well past it, and the pair is then judged at that span. R-K: a correct timer whose reads stop at 0.15 P and resume at 0.97 P is decided at an interval of 97 % of the period. The rule is still sound, by the doubling check alone, as proved under "What holds".
  - **Test evidence is thin where one hidden reload first reads near nominal.** That band starts at about 1.94× (`f > 2/1.03`).
    - `no_hidden_reload_passes_below_twenty_times_fast` starts at 2.2×, and its held read is always read 2, with no stretch before it.
    - Below 2.2×, the randomized model stands in with about 80 of its 1,500 cases between 1.92× and 2.2×. Their holds are random, and few land at the phase that reads nominal.
- **Fix:**
  - Replace the 2.06 / 0.9 sentence, in the code and in ADR-0013, with the doubling argument: a hidden reload makes the true fall at least twice the observed one.
  - Add R-B's construction as a host test, from 1.9× up, with a stretch before and after the held read and samples at either end of their brackets: a hold timed so that the pair reads 97 % to 103 % of nominal.

### T78-R7 (Low): a count that pauses is judged slow at once, and the longer pairs widen the exposure

- **Where:** `judge_pair` (`apic.rs:513-539`) and the continuation toward the reload (`:479`).
- **What:** this lies outside I2's model (a count falling linearly), but the proof is Refuse class and a `Slow` pair fails the run at once, with no repeat.
  - **How it happens.** Suppose an emulated count does not fall linearly right up to the reload: for example, it stays at 0 after an expiry until the emulation re-arms, or at the initial count after a reload. Then a pair through the pause measures a fall too short for its time, and its bounds lie wholly above 103 %.
  - **R-L.** With a pause at 0 after each expiry:

    | Brackets | Pause | Phases judged `Slow` |
    |---|---|---|
    | 5 µs | 60 µs | 12 of 100 |
    | 5 µs | 1 ms | 19 of 100 |
    | 120 µs | 1 ms | 41 of 100 |

    The 120 µs cases are new: a wide pair now goes on toward the reload, whereas before this change such a pair was never clean.
  - **The stretch rule does not see it.** With short steps, the pause lies inside one stretch, whose slope agrees with the pair's.
  - **Whether any hypervisor does this.** I do not know; I read no hypervisor source. The base's KVM record argues against a long pause on the runners that decided: the EPYC 9V74 decided at its first pair in 31 of 31 boots, and about a fifth of those pairs would have crossed an expiry.
- **Fix:** keep the lowest upper slope bound over the pair's single steps, and set the pair aside when the pair's own lower bound exceeds it: some step fell less than its time allows. Every step's interval holds the true rate of a linear count, so this never sets a correct timer aside.
  - In the port, this guard turned every `Slow` of R-L into `InBounds`.
  - On 300 linear-count cases with stalls it changed nothing: 297 decided, none wrong.
  - Either way, add a paused-count host test.

### T78-R8 (Low): `MOST_READS`' figure is wrong by orders of magnitude where reads are slow

- **Where:** `apic.rs:395`: "about 0.5 s of reads at 30 ns each."
- **What:** a fenced x2APIC read is RDTSCP, LFENCE, RDMSR and RDTSCP. Under KVM the RDMSR exits, which costs microseconds, up to the tens of microseconds the 8370C seems to take. At those costs 2^24 reads take about 17 s at 1 µs each, and about 8 minutes at 30 µs, per run, for up to four runs. The cap binds only when the TSC does not pass the 128 ms budget (a stopped or very slow TSC), which the calibration makes unlikely. So this is about the bound as stated, not a live risk.
- **Fix:** state the bound honestly ("2^24 reads, whatever each costs"), or lower it to the scale of the TSC budget. For example, 2^21 is still more reads than a 128 ms run makes at 100 ns each.

### T78-R9 (Low): two figures say less than their docs claim

- **`best_ppm`.** Its doc says "the deciding pair's, if one came" (`apic.rs:670-672`). It is in fact the least uncertainty of every pair `judge_pair` saw, set-aside and undecided pairs included, because `figures.closed` runs before the aside and doubling checks (`:527`). R-E: in T78-1's shape, the run decides `Fast` from a pair of ±258 ppm, but `best` reads ±89 ppm, which is the set-aside held pair's.
- **`wide`.** It counts two different kinds of pair together:
  - pairs whose bounds straddled 97 % or 103 %;
  - pairs given up because their stretches never measured the slope, every step being over P/20.

  The second kind is the 300 µs case, and telling the two apart is how a CI log would show "reads cost over about 245 µs" rather than "reads cost 200 µs and the timer sits near a bound". The `steps to` figure hints at it, but does not say which pairs.
- **Fix:** call `figures.closed` only for a pair that reached its verdict, or only for the deciding pair and rename the figure; and count "unmeasured" pairs separately. The line is 158 of 160 bytes at its widest, so a shorter word than `spoiled:` would make room.

### T78-R10 (Info): the gap rule is no longer needed for soundness below 20 times fast

A gap over P/100 resets the pair (`apic.rs:456`). Since C4, a gap could instead close the open stretch, as a long step does. A hidden reload in the gap then lowers the pair's slope, and the doubling check refuses to judge it (the same proof). That would keep pairs alive across stalls on the 65- and 67-processor machines, whose widest gaps were 0.95 to 11.8 ms in the ledger's table. This is a choice for the coordinating session, not a defect.

### T78-R11 (Info): smaller record and test points

- **"Held off for over 475 ms"** (`apic.rs:429-431` and ADR-0013): 50 interrupts in under 25 ms reach 475 ms with about 450 ms held off, so the figure should be about 450 ms.
- **`sample_period`'s doc** says it returns "how many pairs were given up before it". It returns the given-up pairs plus the deciding one (`tried + 1`), which is what the lines print as "from N pairs".
- **`a_randomized_model_never_judges_a_rate_wrong`** draws `ppm` from 50,000 (`apic.rs:1596`, `:1598`). That is exactly 20 times fast, where a step of P/20 can hide a reload: the guarantee holds only below 20·(1 − 1/N). The test passes, but its range should start above 50,000.
- **One phase each.** `wide_reads_decide_and_a_rate_six_percent_out_never_passes` uses one phase per case, so its "never" rests on the randomized model.
- **The 300 µs case's comment** (`apic.rs:1282`) names one of its two reasons. With 300 µs brackets, every step also exceeds P/20, so no stretch ever forms.
- **Where the plants sample.** Every boot plant samples the count at its bracket's start: `controls.py` reads the count right after `before`, then holds the bracket open. The host tests cover the middle, the end and random placements (R-F also checks the end). A plant that holds the bracket open before the RDMSR would cover the other end at boot. T78-2a duplicates T66-30 (both are `wide=30`), so one of them could take that placement.
- **The contract text.** TASKS item 78's contract still says "§13 (no `unsafe` added)", and its I7 says "no `unsafe` added". `946269e4` adds two `unsafe` blocks to `cpu.rs`, which the brief's I7 allows. The records should say so.
