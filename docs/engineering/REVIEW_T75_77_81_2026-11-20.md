## Summary

The three mechanisms are, as far as I can see, correct in their core: `Contexts::withdraw` and `switch` race on one compare-and-swap of the frame word and the claim/withdraw orderings hold in the C++20 model; `resume` now names RSP0 and the kernel-stack record from the table's own answer, and `check_landing` cannot fire on a correct kernel; `Backlog::drained` lowers after its release, and an Acquire 0 synchronizes with every earlier lowering. I found no memory-safety defect.

What is wrong is mostly in the evidence and in one ordering the item did not move:

- **R81-1 (Medium):** `list_closed` still raises the count *after* releasing the list's lock. A reap can take and drain a channel before its own raise, and its lowering then cancels another listed channel's count. A settle can then read 0 while a channel whose close it saw is still listed with its messages allocated: 81-I1 fails in a reachable interleaving, the T79-6 class this item exists to close. B1 cannot see it (one piece). Fix: raise the count under the list's lock.
- **R77-1 (Medium):** because `resume`, not `follow_thread`, now moves the kernel-stack record, the record still names the held thread's own stack in both of the kernel-stacks proof's windows. Clauses W1 and W2 ("in use: true") now pass by the record alone and no longer test the home word (TASKS 70's control W-1 would pass them). The docs and the printed line added in `922cba29` say the opposite ("named by neither the frame nor the record, its home alone covering it").
- **R75-1 (Medium):** `prove_spawn_refused` now requires `stack::counts().lost` unchanged, but that count also rises when any processor's reclaim shootdown stalls (every AP's idle loop reclaims). On the large TCG machines this is a new way to refuse a boot with "scheduler failed", outside ADR-0013 Amendment 3's forgiveness.
- Low findings on capacity documentation (in-flight publications now sit in the table outside the run queue's bound), a few inaccurate sentences, the landing check's real cost, RSP0 = 0, the settle's wait order, the reap fast path's new lock traffic, and a misplaced doc comment in `loom.rs`.

## Item 75: a refused spawn takes nothing into the run queue

### R75-1 (Medium): the new "no stack lost" clauses fail on a stalled shootdown, not only on a leak

- **Where:** `framekernel/src/sched.rs:1324` (`let lost = || origin_frame::stack::counts().lost`), used at `:1354`, `:1367` and `:1381`.
- **What is wrong:** `stack::counts().lost` is the region's `Counts::lost`, which `Slots::leak` raises (the drop's leak, what the clause means to catch) and which `Slots::reclaim` also raises when a shootdown stalls (`slots.rs:391–392`; `stack::reclaim`'s `Some(Err(_)) => done.lost += 1`). Between `lost_before` and the last read, the proof calls `yield_until`, which calls `reap()` → `reclaim_stacks()` every round, and the final `reap()`; and every AP runs `run_ap`'s `loop { reap(); cpu::idle(); }`, so any retired stack anywhere is reclaimed, with a shootdown to every processor, while this proof runs.
- **Failure scenario:** boot-matrix on the 65/67-processor TCG machines under host load (the class of TASKS 66 and 69). Stacks of the preceding proofs' threads are retired; an AP's idle `reap()` reclaims one; the shootdown stalls one second on a descheduled vCPU; the slot becomes `Lost`. `prove_spawn_refused` then prints `...: scheduler failed`. `lost_stacks_only` (`main.rs:2763`) qualifies only Refuse count lines whose settle words name lost stacks, so this boot is refused, not re-run or forgiven (ADR-0013 Amendment 3). Before item 75 this proof had no such clause.
- **Fix:** test the refused stacks' own slots, as TASKS 70's W3 does (`stack::slot_state(slot) != Some(State::Lost)`, ruling (d) there): capture `stack.slot()` in the injected publisher (case 3) and through `spawn_with_parts` with a wrapping publisher for case 2, then read those slots' states after the proof. Or subtract the losses `reclaim_stacks` reports, which `report_lost_stacks` already prints.

### R75-2 (Low): the table's capacity argument no longer holds; a full table can now come from spawns the queue would refuse

- **Where:** `framekernel/frame/sync/src/contexts.rs:119–128` ("A processor holds at most one context per run-queue slot … That is 17 of 32"), `framekernel/frame/src/cpu.rs` `CONTEXTS`' doc ("at most one context per slot of its run queue … 17 of … 32"), `framekernel/src/sched.rs:747` ("which has 15 to spare … a table filled that way refuses another spawn, which is safe").
- **What is wrong:** every spawn now holds a table slot from its publication, outside any lock and with interrupts on, until it is queued or withdrawn: successful spawns waiting for the queue lock and doomed ones alike. The bound is now "queue contexts + 1 ended + spawns in flight to this processor", and a spawner can be preempted in that stretch, so spawns in flight are bounded only by the threads that can be spawning at once (up to 15 per processor, times the processors). The old order published under the queue lock after the push, so a doomed spawn never touched the table.
- **Failure scenario:** 16 or more spawns targeting one processor are in flight (many threads of user processes calling the thread-start system call for the same processor on a large machine, some preempted after publishing). A spawn that the queue would accept is refused `Full` by the table; and a processor whose idle flow has not yet made its first switch-out would get `Refused::Full` from `switch`, and `resume` panics ("too many saved thread frames"). I found no path in today's boot that reaches it (before `GO` only the boot flow spawns), so this is a documentation and margin finding.
- **Fix:** restate the capacity rule with the in-flight term, and either say why it cannot reach 16 or reserve the boot/idle flow's slot (claim it when the processor's table is set up, not at its first switch-out). The sched.rs sentence should name the halt, not only "refuses another spawn".

### R75-3 (Low): one sentence of the new ordering rules is false as written

- **Where:** `contexts.rs:41–42`: "only a claimer that has just taken a free slot writes a frame into one whose `frame` is 0".
- **What is wrong:** `switch` also writes `here` into the outgoing context's slot, whose `frame` is 0 while it runs (`Some(slot) => slot.frame.store(here, Release)`). The withdrawal argument still holds, because a withdrawn context is never `current`, so no switch-out writes its slot; but that is the reason, and the doc gives another one.
- **Fix:** "only a claimer that has just taken a free slot, or this processor's switch-out of the context it runs (`current`), writes a frame into a slot whose `frame` is 0; a withdrawn context is never `current`."

### R75-4 (Low): the W1 doc comment is attached to the wrong constant in `loom.rs`

- **Where:** `developer-tools/xtask/src/loom.rs:134–149`.
- **What is wrong:** the `///` lines describing W1's reports precede `BACKLOG_EARLY` together with B1's, so rustdoc gives both to `BACKLOG_EARLY` and `CTX_WITHDRAWN` (W1's list) has none.
- **Fix:** move W1's two lines above `CTX_WITHDRAWN`.

### Sound in item 75

- **Every failure after the publication withdraws before the stack drops.** After `publish` succeeds the only fallible step is `push_weighted`; on its `Err`, `parts` was never taken, so the `Err(_)` arm moves the stack out, withdraws, and drops it at the end of the `if let`; the FPU area and the `UserContext` are dropped after, outside the queue lock (they were dropped inside the lock with interrupts off before: an improvement). A refused publication returns before `parts` exists. `THREADS` missing now refuses before a stack is made.
- **`withdraw` against `switch`, `publish`, `retire_ended`, `covers`.** One compare-and-swap of the frame word decides between withdraw and switch (strong CAS, no spurious failure). The claimer's AcqRel CAS of `home` reads withdraw's Release store, so withdraw's frame CAS happens before the claimer's frame store, and coherence orders them; in fact the RMW atomicity of the frame CAS alone already prevents a claimer's frame from being lost. `retire_ended` touches only `ended`'s slot, never a withdrawn one; `covers` keeps finding the home until the Release store, and the only holder that drops the stack is the withdrawer, sequenced after it.
- **Misuse is safe, more than the doc says.** The doc says `withdraw` "cannot tell a context that ran and is suspended at exactly its first frame's address". It never has to: in 64-bit mode every interrupt delivery aligns RSP to 16 before pushing, privilege change or not (SDM §7.14.2), so every saved frame is at `16k − FRAME_WORDS·8`, while a first frame is at `top − 8 − FRAME_WORDS·8` with `top` page-aligned, 8 apart modulo 16. A withdrawal of a stack already in the scheduler's hands therefore fails, and the stack leaks rather than being freed. Worth one sentence in the doc.
- **75-I1:** the slot becomes `Ready` only after publication; `follow_thread`'s `UNREADY_SWITCHES` still sees a whole thread because `Threads` is written under the queue lock before release.
- **W1 and its controls.** WX1 (plain store) gives "both or neither"; WX2 (home first) lets C claim the slot between the two stores and either loses A's frame ("neither") or clears a resumed A's home ("uncovered"). Both are reachable in W1's three-thread exhaustive search. The contract's concurrent `covers` reader was replaced by the publisher (addition (b)); nothing is lost, since no other party drops the stack.
- **No new `unsafe`; no `asm!` added** (`models.rs` is on the `unsafe` list; `backlog.rs` and `contexts.rs` forbid it).

## Item 77: the framework names the running context's own stack

### R77-1 (Medium): the kernel-stacks proof's switch windows no longer test the home word, and the new text says they do

- **Where:** `framekernel/src/stacks.rs:14–24` (module docs), `:528–533` (`hold_in_window`'s doc), `:693` (the printed line); `developer-tools/xtask/src/main.rs:3220` (the boot test's marker). The cause is in `framekernel/src/sched.rs:129–147` (`follow_thread` no longer moves the record) and `framekernel/frame/src/cpu.rs:886–891` (`resume` moves it after `switch`).
- **What is wrong:** before item 77, `with_queue` called `follow_thread`, which moved the processor's kernel-stack record to the incoming context's stack (or 0) *before* `on_sleep`/`on_exit` called `hold_in_window`. So in both windows the outgoing thread's stack was named by no record but its home, which is what clauses W1 and W2 were built to show (TASKS 70; T68-2). Now the record moves only in `resume`, after the hooks return. While the processor is held, its `CpuLocal.kernel_stack` still equals `slot_top(home) = top(S)` of the held thread. The boot flow's `slot_in_use(slot)` → `stack_in_use(low, low + SLOT, top = low + SLOT)` compares every processor's record with `top` exactly and answers `true` from the record alone, in the switch-out window and in the exit window.
- **Failure scenario:** TASKS 70's control W-1 (`covers` reads no home) failed W1 and W2 with `in use: false` (ADR-0013's controls table). Re-run on this tip, W1 and W2 would print `in use: true` and pass; only R2 (the measured-stack proof) would still catch W-1. Any future regression that drops homes in a switch window would pass the clauses that exist to catch it. Meanwhile the new docs and line state "its stack named by neither the frame nor the record, its home alone covering it", which is false.
- **Fix:** either ask a table-only question in the windows (a framework query such as `stack::slot_in_contexts(slot)` reading `CONTEXTS[*].covers` only, kept beside `slot_in_use`), or hold where the home is now alone: after `resume` has named the incoming stack on the exit path (the stretch from the exit's switch to the next retirement), which needs a framework hook. Then correct the three texts and the marker, and run W-1 again to record what it now gives.

### R77-2 (Low): the landing check costs more than the contract's estimate, on every system call

- **Where:** `framekernel/frame/src/cpu.rs:787–806` (`check_landing`), called from `dispatch` for every ring-3 entry without IST.
- **What is wrong:** the contract and Q5 put it at "two loads, two subtractions and a compare". The code does `this_cpu()` (a GS load), a `PerCpu` lookup of `CONTEXTS`, the `current` load, the home load (Acquire), then `slot_holding` twice, each calling `region()` (an Acquire load of `STACK_REGION_BASE` and, the base being placed, a load of `STACK_REGION_DRAWN` it does not need) and `region_size()` (an Acquire load), a subtraction and a division by `SLOT` (a shift), then a second lookup of `CPU_LOCAL` and a load and store of the counter. About a dozen loads per system call and per user interrupt. Cheap, but CL43 should be the measured figure, not the estimate.
- **Fix:** read the base and the size once and compare `(here − base) >> log2(SLOT)` with `(home − base) >> log2(SLOT)`; count with the `CpuLocal` already looked up. Measure and record CL43 from that.

### R77-3 (Low): RSP0 = 0 for the boot or idle flow wraps to the top of the kernel window instead of faulting at once

- **Where:** `cpu.rs:734–763` (`name_kernel_stack(BOOT_FLOW)` writes 0 to the record and to TSS.RSP0).
- **What is wrong:** if a kernel bug ever let the boot or idle flow reach ring 3, the next entry from ring 3 would push its frame below 0, at `0xFFFF_FFFF_FFFF_FFF8` downward, inside `KERNEL_WINDOW` (`0xFFFF_FFFF_8000_0000`, 2 GiB). If that top page is unmapped this is a #PF during delivery, then #DF on its IST stack and a reported halt; if anything is ever mapped there, it is silent corruption. I did not verify that the top page stays unmapped. The old behaviour (stale top of the last thread's stack) was worse, so this is a hardening note.
- **Fix:** document that the top page of the kernel window must stay unmapped, or name an address whose use faults deterministically (a non-canonical value would make the stack switch fault rather than wrap).

### R77-4 (Low, scope note): only the stack is taken from the table; the address space is still the scheduler's

- **Where:** `sched.rs:129–147` (`follow_thread` activates `t.user[slot]`'s space from the scheduler's slot).
- **What is wrong:** the contract's security line says 77 removes "a way for a scheduler error to reach the trap entry's state". For the stack, yes. But a scheduler whose `current()` slot disagreed with the frame it answered would still have `follow_thread` load another process's CR3 for the context the table resumes. The landing check would not catch that. Not a defect of this change; worth recording as the next gap of the same kind.
- **Fix:** none required here; a follow-up to name the address space from the table (or check it against the resumed context) would close the same class for CR3.

### Sound in item 77

- **No false positive on a correct kernel.** `current` is written only by this processor's `switch`, before `resume` returns, with interrupts off until IRETQ. Every entry from ring 3 therefore finds `current` = the context that was resumed. Its home is in its own stack, and `slot_top(home)` equals `GuardedStack::top()` (a stack sits at the top of its slot), so the CPU's pushes at RSP0 land in the home's slot. The syscall frame is built at `gs:[16]`, the same value. IST vectors (1, 2, 8, 18, as `init` installs them through the same `ist_of`) are skipped, matching §7.14.5: an IST gate switches stacks unconditionally. #DF's undefined CS is never read. The boot or idle flow never reaches ring 3, so its `BOOT_FLOW` home (in no slot) never faces the check. A context suspended on a measured stack is named by its home, which is right: it can return to ring 3 only through frames on its own stack.
- **A wrong RSP0 cannot pass.** The source is the table (`running_home`), never the record or RSP0 (addition (c)). A wrong RSP0 or record lands the frame in another slot, and the check halts. C77b's case (record right, RSP0 stale) fails on the first interrupt from ring 3. The check runs after the stub's pushes, so the other stack is already written to when it halts; since contexts never move between processors, halting this processor keeps the damaged context from resuming.
- **Every switch path** (tick, reschedule, device, yield, sleep, block, exit, system-call exit, user fault including #DB on its IST stack, the boot flow's first switch-out and its switch back) goes through `resume`; `next == here` names nothing and leaves the record correct; an exit names the incoming context, and the ended stack stays covered by its home until the next retirement. NMI and #MC between the switch and IRETQ come from ring 0 on IST stacks and do not switch. 77-I3 holds: the record only ever names the home of `current`.
- **K1 and KX1–KX4** catch what they aim at (KX1 fails at the first switch with `None`, KX2 at the resume of a saved frame, KX3/KX4 at the switch to B). `CpuLocal` stays `repr(C, align(64))` at 56 bytes, so the asm offsets 0–24 are unchanged.
- **SDM citations** match the September 2026 edition: §7.14.4 "Stack Switching in IA-32e Mode", §7.14.5 "Interrupt Stack Table", §6.8.8 "Fast System Calls in 64-Bit Mode", §10.7 "Task Management in 64-bit Mode".

## Item 81: a settle waits for a reap in progress

### R81-1 (Medium): a raise made after the list's lock lets another channel's lowering cancel it; a settle can read 0 with a seen channel still listed

- **Where:** `framekernel/src/ipc.rs:456–466` (`list_closed`: push under `CLOSED`'s lock, then `CLOSED_COUNT.added()` after the lock is released); `framekernel/frame/sync/src/backlog.rs:10` and `:42–44` (the rule "`added`: after a piece is listed" and "a drain with no listing behind it would wrap the count … never early").
- **What is wrong:** the count is a sum. A reap takes the head of the list under the lock and lowers after its drain. Nothing orders a channel's raise before its own take, because the raise is outside the lock, so a reap can lower for channel Y before Y's lister raised for it. That lowering then cancels a raise that belongs to a different channel X, still listed. The backlog doc considers only a drain with no listing behind it, which wraps the count; the case here does not wrap.
- **Failure scenario** (the list is LIFO: a push goes on the head, a reap takes the head):
  1. CPU A, the last thread of a proof's process P ends: `close_out` → `close_side` lists X (messages still queued to P) and raises. List [X], count 1.
  2. CPU B, a thread of a live process Q closes an end that has undelivered messages: `list_closed` pushes Y under the lock (list [Y, X]); `without_interrupts` returns, and the timer interrupt that arrived during the critical section switches Q's thread out before `added()`. Count still 1.
  3. CPU A, P's `close_out` then calls `reap_closed` (any system-call epilogue on another processor would do the same): it sees 1, takes the head, **Y**, drains it and lowers the count to 0; the loop sees 0 and returns, leaving **X listed** with its messages.
  4. P publishes its end (`ending`, then the thread's exit). The boot flow's `settle` sees the end; its closure's `reap_closed()` sees 0 and returns at once without looking at the list; `closed_pending() == 0`; the settle passes and `judge` reads `closed` 0.
  5. The proof takes its first count with X's messages, and everything they hold, still allocated. When Q's thread runs again it raises the count to 1, and some later reap frees X. The proof's compare is then off by those messages ("heap X (was Y)"): the T79-6 failure, through an interleaving item 81 leaves open.

  The window is not a few instructions: `added()` runs right after `without_interrupts` re-enables interrupts, which is exactly where an interrupt that arrived during the critical section is taken. On QEMU's software CPU a descheduled vCPU stretches it further.
- **Fix:** raise under the lock, inside `list_closed`'s `without_interrupts` closure, before the lock is released. Then every take of a channel happens after its raise (the lock orders them), each lowering follows its own raise in the count's modification order, the count never falls below the number of channels listed or in their drain, and a 0 read by Acquire means the list was empty and no drain was in progress at that point. State the rule in `backlog.rs` ("`added` must happen before any taker can take the piece, for example under the list's lock"), drop the "wrap … never early" sentence, and add a two-piece model, with a control that moves the raise outside the lock. The model I prepared is one (see "What I could not check"; it has not run).

### R81-2 (Low): the closed-channel wait runs after the exits wait, and its own drains can start ends the judge then reads

- **Where:** `framekernel/src/sched.rs:383–421` (`settle`: exits wait, stacks, then `closed channels' reaping`, then `judge` reads `exits_pending()` again).
- **What is wrong:** the settle's closure is now the one that drains whatever is still listed. Dropping undelivered messages closes the ends they carry, which wakes waiters and port sources (`close_side` → `signals_changed`). A woken thread whose peer is gone may end at once. It ends after the exits wait has passed, and `judge` reads `exits.ending`/`zombies` after the closed wait, so the settle can fail ("1 ending threads not yet exited") because of what it just did itself. The class existed before through other reapers; Q9 makes the settle itself one of them.
- **Failure scenario:** a channel is still listed at the settle (any close not followed by its own reap, or R81-1's leftover); one of its messages carries the last handle to an end that a service thread is blocked on; the settle's drain wakes the service, which exits; `judge` sees it ending.
- **Fix:** put the closed-channel wait before the exits wait (nothing the exits or stacks waits do lists a channel except through an end, which `end_with` already covers), or repeat the three waits until one pass changes nothing.

### R81-3 (Low): the fast path now takes a global lock on every system call while any drain is in progress

- **Where:** `ipc.rs:476–501`, called from every system call's epilogue (`syscall.rs:95`), with interrupts on.
- **What is wrong:** before, the count fell when a channel was taken, so other processors' epilogues skipped the lock. Now it stays raised for the whole drain, and a drain in an epilogue runs with interrupts on and can be preempted in the middle. For that whole time every system call on every processor takes `CLOSED`'s spin lock with interrupts off, finds the list empty and returns. The contract's performance line ("the same number of read-modify-writes, reordered") misses this.
- **Fix:** record it in the cost ledger. If it matters, make the fast check "listed" rather than "listed or draining" (a second count for the list alone), or have `reap_closed` skip the lock when the list head is observed empty without it. That is Q8's trade-off, revisited with this cost.

### Sound in item 81

- **81-I1, apart from R81-1.** `drained` runs the release, then an AcqRel `fetch_sub`. Every change of the count is an RMW, so each lowering heads a release sequence that contains all later RMWs. An Acquire load that reads 0 (or any later value) synchronizes with every lowering before it in modification order, on any processor ([intro.races], [atomics.order]). The reasoning in `backlog.rs`'s "The ordering" is right.
- **81-I2.** The list's lock, the `listed` swap and its comment are unchanged; the drain moved into the closure with the same message take under each slot's lock. A close that lists the channel again during a drain raises before the drain's lowering, so the count does not dip. The list's own reference is dropped inside the closure, so a channel freed by the reap is freed before the lowering.
- **A reap finding a count while another drains** takes the lock once and returns; nested `reap_closed` calls (none from `Drop`; `close_out` and `abandon` are explicit calls) terminate the same way.
- **Addition (e).** The closure runs on the boot flow with interrupts off between interrupt windows and holds no lock across a drop; `UserSpace::drop` leaks a space still loaded and frees an unloaded one without a shootdown; cross-processor wake-ups write the ICR and do not wait. I found no wait on another processor in the drop paths I read. (Reaps in system-call epilogues already ran these drops, with interrupts on.)
- **B1 and BX1–BX3** catch what they aim at for one piece: BX1 reproduces T79-6, BX2 and BX3 break the synchronization Loom checks with the cell.
- `settled.rs`'s order (`ending, zombies, retired, closed, lost`) leaves the place item 73's `waiting_for` needs; `closed_channels_do_not_qualify` covers the new words in `lost_stacks_only`.

- Item 75's ordering and withdrawal, item 77's choice of stack and its check, and item 81's release-then-lower are each right in their core mechanism (the "Sound" lists above).
- No new file holds `unsafe`; the three `unsafe` blocks added in `models.rs` are in a listed file and documented; the TSS write did not move out of `cpu.rs`; no `asm!` was added or changed.

## What I could not check

- **The Loom experiment for R81-1.** I wrote a two-piece model in a copy of `target/loom-scratch` in my scratchpad (`loomx/`, `run_loomx.sh`): the same `Backlog`, a LIFO list under the crate's `SpinLock`, piece X listed and raised first, a second lister that pushes Y and raises after its lock, an ending thread that reaps and then publishes its end, and a settler that reaps and, with nothing pending, reads X's cell. One variant raises after the lock (today's `list_closed`), the other under it (the fix). **It did not run.** It waited for the machine's turn behind another session's multi-round boot-matrix diagnostic (`xenodochial-shannon-859a93`, TASKS 73's `diag_round.sh idle,matrix,load`), and I stopped it before it could take a turn nobody would read.
