## What they show

**A binding is one-shot and level-triggered wherever it is the default; the descriptions differ on what a close does.** Fuchsia's binding fires once, and if the signal is already active when the binding is made a packet is queued at once (an edge option changes that; C9 has no edge). When the bound handle is closed, Fuchsia ends its pending waits silently and keeps packets already queued. kqueue removes a closed descriptor's events silently. epoll is level-triggered by default, has `EPOLLONESHOT`, and removes a registration only when every descriptor of the same open file description is closed, so a duplicate keeps events arriving for a descriptor the program has closed; the page describes that as a surprise to guard against. Windows keeps a handle associated until it is closed, and advises against duplicating an associated handle because operations through the duplicate notify the queue too. illumos does not say what closing the descriptor does. **None of them delivers a packet for the close; C9 does.**

**A wait on a queue whose handle is closed fails, where the case is described at all.** `GetQueuedCompletionStatus` fails with `ERROR_ABANDONED_WAIT_0` and returns no packet. Fuchsia's `zx_port_wait` page does not address it. On illumos, closing the port removes its associations.

**A cancel that matches nothing says so.** `zx_port_cancel` answers `ZX_ERR_NOT_FOUND`; kqueue answers `ENOENT` when the event to change or delete is not there; `port_dissociate` answers `ENOENT`, and `EACCES` to a caller that does not own the association. C11 gave Origin `not_found` for this.

**Binding the same thing twice has every answer.** Fuchsia does not deduplicate: two bindings, two packets. kqueue identifies a registration by its (ident, filter) pair, and adding it again modifies it. illumos updates the events and the user value. epoll refuses with `EEXIST`.

**The packet.** Fuchsia's is a `u64` key, a `u32` type, an `i32` status and a 32-byte union; for a signal the union holds `trigger` (the signals asked), `observed` (the signals active when the packet was made), a count and an optional timestamp. C9's is `{key; kind; trigger; observed; reserved; data}`. The pages do not say when a packet's storage is taken: `ZX_ERR_NO_MEMORY` is among the binding call's errors and not among `zx_port_wait`'s, and that is all they show. C9 says it outright: reserved at bind time.

**Order and bounds.** Fuchsia's packets leave in the order they were queued. Windows queues in that order, says packets may be taken in another, and releases waiting threads last-in first-out. Three of the systems bound registrations per user, per process or per port: epoll's `max_user_watches`, kqueue's `RLIMIT_KQUEUES` (reported as `ENOMEM`), illumos's `process.max-port-events` (`EAGAIN`). Fuchsia's pages name no bound.

**Events.** `zx_event_create` takes an options word that must be 0, the shape D1 decided against. `zx_object_signal` clears first and then sets, needs the signal right, and does not say what two empty masks do. kqueue's `EVFILT_USER` is an event a program raises itself, queued like any other.

**The clock.** `zx_clock_get_monotonic` takes no argument, needs no right and cannot fail. POSIX's `CLOCK_MONOTONIC` counts from an unspecified point that does not change after start-up and cannot be set; the page read neither says in so many words that it never steps back nor whether two readings may be equal. ADR-0012 C1 says both for Origin's clock: never less, possibly equal.

**Ring 3 already has a counter.** With CR4.TSD clear, RDTSC and RDTSCP run at any privilege level; with it set they are ring 0 only and fault in ring 3 (Intel and AMD agree). Neither instruction is serializing. RDTSCP also returns IA32_TSC_AUX (`TSC_AUX`, MSR C000_0103h), and RDPID reads that register at any level whatever TSD says. The counter increases monotonically except at wraparound; where the invariant counter is reported (CPUID 8000_0007h EDX bit 8) both vendors call it usable as a time source, and CPUID leaf 15H gives its ratio to the crystal on Intel. The SDM adds that a system which sets TSD should give programs an interface that emulates the instruction (§20.17).

## What this means for Origin

1. **The interface.** Seven calls. Numbers 9 and 10 lose `@reserved` and gain their errors; five calls continue from 15. One struct (`port_packet`), one enum (`packet_kind`) and two rights constants. Every addition carries `@since(0.6)`, and `origin.kernel` and `user-abi` go 0.5 → 0.6 in one step with the fingerprint lock re-recorded against the amendment that records the shapes. None of that is touched before the owner accepts the shapes (§84).
2. **Two objects.** `Object::Event` and `Object::Port` join `ipc.rs`'s four. An event is one word of signals and a place its waiters and bindings hang from. A port is a queue of packets, its waiting threads and a closed mark, behind an end that counts handles as `ChannelEnd` does, so the kernel knows when the last handle goes.
3. **Every signal change delivers.** Signals change in three places: a channel's state (`ipc.rs`, `wake(channel, side)`), a process's or thread's end (`Termination::raise`, `process.rs:236`), and now an event. Each keeps its order — the state first, then the wake — and adds the delivery to bindings after it. Delivery allocates nothing: a binding's storage was made at bind time and only moves.
4. **Three places cancel implicitly:** `handle_close` (`ipc.rs:632`), the handles a successful `channel_write` moves (`ipc.rs:502`; its list of moved objects must also carry each handle's value), and exit. Exit is the awkward one: `close_out` replaces the table whole and an `Object` does not know its handle's value, so exit either walks the old table's entries or cancels by owner process.
5. **Storage and quota.** `object_wait_async` reserves one packet's storage and charges the binder before the binding exists; both can fail (`limit`, `no_memory`) and a failure changes nothing. After that no path allocates.
6. **Locks.** `ipc.rs`'s written order is: a handle table, then `TRANSFERS`, then a channel's state, then its charge list, then an end's holder link; a wait queue's lock before whatever its condition reads; a waker holds none of them while it wakes. S3-6 adds two locks and must add them to that paragraph: an object's list of bindings, which sits where a wait queue's lock sits (taken before a table or a channel's state is read), and a port's queue, taken after it and never before. No wake-up under either; closing a port never recurses into another close.
7. **Sixteen is not a bound on bindings.** A `WaitQueue` holds 16 waiters. Bindings do not live in one; their number is bounded by the binder's quota. Threads blocked in `port_wait` do live in one, and past 16 they poll by yielding as every wait does (`THREAT_MODEL_SYSCALLS.md`, row V3).
8. **Nothing new is indexed by a ring-3 value** (ADR-0017 Z9). Handles already pass `nospec` in the handle table. A key is compared and never hashed or used as an index. Masks are bit operations, a deadline is compared, and the packet's copy-out is a fixed 32 bytes. Each goes into the threat model's Z9 list as looked at, with why it is not a lookup.
9. **The clock.** `clock_read` returns `time::monotonic_nanos()`: the reading passes ADR-0012's floor, so no thread on any processor sees it step back, and two readings may be equal. It is the same clock deadlines are on, and the first way a program can form a deadline that is neither 0 nor infinite. What it gives ring 3 beyond what RDTSC, RDTSCP and RDPID already give (CR4.TSD is clear today and no code touches it): nanoseconds since the kernel's start, already scaled by the kernel's calibration, and one order across processors. It gives nothing finer than the counter. It also lets ring 3 raise the shared floor as often as it can call, and ADR-0012 measured what that contention costs (2 ns for one reader, 582 ns each for ten); the floor is lock-free, so that slows readers and blocks none. If TSD is ever set, `clock_read` is the interface the SDM asks for, and its resolution becomes a decision then.
10. **No kernel address and no reference leaves the kernel.** A packet holds what the binder gave (key, mask) and signals. What `port_cancel` matches an object by is a number from a kernel counter that never repeats, never shown to ring 3.
11. **`#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` on every new kernel file.** If the protocol needs `unsafe` (intrusive lists), it is the framework's to hold (CLAUDE.md §13, decision item 111).
12. **The proof needs no new program, and it is two results.** The script interpreter passes 64-bit immediates and loaded handles and reports bytes of the program's memory, which covers every line below. The proof is a new file, `framekernel/src/ipc_proof/events_ports.rs`, with two rows in `bootproof.rs` (61 proofs become 63), two rows in ADR-0013's table and markers in xtask. ADR-0013 R1 splits a result that mixes its two kinds. Delivery, cancellation, the quotas, the hostile calls (a bad pointer to `clock_read` among them) and the leak line are P0 and structural: **Refuse**. That the clock's readings are in order is not a P0 property: the table's row for the monotonic clock is Report, and gives as a reason that nothing P0 rests on the clock's order. So the clock as ring 3 reads it is a **Report** result of its own.
13. **E1 comes after.** When handles become 64-bit, these calls' handle arguments widen with the rest. The packet carries no handle, so it does not change.

## What the kernel needs from the framework

For the framework's session, as task S3-6a. The protocol that binds, raises, cancels and closes is expected to sit where wait queues sit: in the framework's sync crate (a new file beside `framekernel/frame/sync/src/wait.rs`), generic over the scheduler and over what the kernel supplies, so that host tests and Loom run the code that ships, and instantiated by a kernel file as `framekernel/src/wait.rs` instantiates `WaitQueue`. Names, representation and layout are the framework's. What follows is what the kernel must be able to do and to rely on. Nothing under `framekernel/frame/` is this task's to edit, and nothing here asks for a change to `WaitQueue`, the `Scheduler` trait or `UserSpace`.

**Three pieces.**

- A **source**, one per thing that raises signals: a channel's side, a `Termination` (a process's or a thread's), an event. It holds the bindings armed on it, and it has an identity: a number from a counter that never repeats, which ring 3 never sees.
- A **port queue**: packets in the order they were queued, the threads waiting for one, and a closed mark.
- A **reservation**: one binding's storage, of one size, made by a fallible call before any lock is taken. It carries a payload the kernel sets (the binder's account), so that whoever gets the storage back knows whom to refund. It moves from the kernel to a source's armed list, to a port's queue, and back to the kernel, and no move allocates.

**Operations.** The signatures are a sketch of what crosses the line, not a design:

```
Reservation::<P>::try_new(payload: P) -> Result<Reservation<P>, NoMemory>

Source::bind(&self, port: &Port<P>, storage: Reservation<P>, owner: O, key: u64, mask: u32,
             still_open: impl Fn() -> bool, signals: impl Fn() -> u32)
    -> Result<(), (Refused, Reservation<P>)>          // Refused: the handle or the port is gone
Source::deliver(&self, signals: impl Fn() -> u32)     // after every change of the object's state
Source::cancel_owners(&self, which: impl Fn(&O) -> bool, signals: impl Fn() -> u32)
                                                      // implicit: armed -> a `canceled` packet
Source::cancel(&self, port: &Port<P>, key: u64) -> Removed<P>
                                                      // explicit: armed and queued, silent
Port::take(&self, deadline: u64, still_open: impl Fn() -> bool)
    -> Result<(Packet, Reservation<P>), TimedOut | Canceled>
Port::close(&self) -> Removed<P>                      // the last handle went
```

- **bind**, under the source's lock: ask `still_open` (the bound handle is still in the binder's table, as a wait's condition asks), read `signals` fresh, and either queue the packet at once (they meet the mask) or arm the binding. A closed port refuses. A refusal hands the storage back.
- **deliver** fires every armed binding whose mask the fresh signals meet: the binding leaves the armed list and its storage enters its port's queue as a `signal` packet with `trigger` = the mask and `observed` = the signals just read. A port found closed gets nothing, and the storage goes back to the kernel.
- **cancel_owners** turns each armed binding whose owner matches into a `canceled` packet, with the signals read then. `handle_close` and a successful `channel_write` call it for one owner (a process and a handle's value); exit calls it for every binding a process made on each object its table held.
- **cancel** removes every binding armed on this source for that port and key, and every packet queued in that port from this source with that key, of either kind, and says how many. The storage comes back to be freed and refunded outside every lock.
- **take** returns one packet. It asks `still_open` before it takes one, so a wait cancelled by its handle's close consumes nothing. It blocks through the existing wait protocol, so the deadline's resolution and the 16 waiters are that protocol's.
- **close** marks the port closed, wakes its waiters, takes out every queued packet, and removes every binding armed on the port from its source, without searching every object in the system. When it returns, every reservation that was armed on or queued in the port is in the caller's hands, so the charges are refunded before `handle_close` returns.

**What the kernel relies on.**

1. **Nothing allocates after `try_new`.** Whatever grows, grows at bind, fallibly, before the binding exists. Delivery, both cancels, take and close only move storage.
2. **One binding, at most one packet**, on its own port, of kind `signal` or `canceled` and never both.
3. **No delivery is lost.** A signal raised before, during or after a bind queues exactly one packet. The kernel's part: every raiser changes the object's state first and calls `deliver` after, holding none of its own locks.
4. **After an explicit cancel has returned, no packet from a binding it matched exists or appears.**
5. **One lock order.** A source's lock is taken where a wait queue's is: before whatever the closures read (a handle table, a channel's state). A port's queue lock comes after a source's, never before, and is a leaf: nothing is taken under it and no closure runs under it. No thread is woken under either; the threads to wake are collected and woken after, as `take_all` does. A waker holds no wait queue's lock while it takes another.
6. **Close is flat.** It never holds the port's lock while it takes a source's, and returning storage never closes a handle (a reservation's payload is an account, not an object), so a close cannot recurse into another.
7. **Storage comes back exactly once**, with its payload, to code that runs outside every lock.
8. **No bound of the framework's on bindings.** Not a fixed array like a wait queue's 16; the kernel's quota is the bound.
9. **A queued packet holds no counted reference to its object.** It names its source by the number. Otherwise a port's handle queued in a channel, and a packet from that channel queued in the port, would keep each other alive, a cycle A3's check does not see. A packet carries no handle, so a port is a leaf in A3's graph.
10. **An armed binding may hold its port's storage, but not a handle count**, so a port still closes when its last handle goes. The reverse reference (the port's way back to the sources it has bindings on) lasts only while a binding is armed, and a binding is armed only while its handle sits in a live process's table, so it keeps alive nothing that a handle does not already.
11. **The owner is opaque**, `Copy + Eq`. The kernel uses a process's ID (never reused) and a handle's value; E1 widens the value.
12. **No kernel address in a `Packet`**: a key, a kind, two masks.

**Cost.** A delivery walks the source's armed bindings under its lock, and an explicit cancel walks them and the port's queue. Both are bounded by the binders' quotas, and a delivery is paid by whoever raised the signal: a process that shares a channel end can make its peer's writes slower up to its own `bindings` quota. The threat model gets a row for it in stage 2.

**Questions for the framework's session.**

- `ObjectRef` has no weak form. Is the port's way back to its sources a counted reference, on the argument in 10, or does the framework want a weak one?
- Representation: lists threaded through the reservations need `unsafe`, which only the framework may hold; a vector grown at bind is the other way. Either satisfies 1.
- Who gives a source its number: a counter in the framework, or the kernel at construction?
- Exit: the kernel can call `cancel_owners` per object as it empties a dead process's table, matching on the process's ID. If the framework would rather give one call that cancels everything a process bound, that serves as well.

**Loom models**, on the shipping protocol, added with it, each with at least one control committed in xtask's `CONTROLS` (a mutation whose `from` text occurs exactly once and which must make the named model fail). The first five are the task contract's; the last four were found while writing this.

| Model | What must hold | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Bind against raise | Exactly one packet, whichever comes first | The bind reads the signals before it takes the source's lock: a raise in between is lost |
| Raise against explicit cancel | When the cancel has returned, no packet is queued and none arrives; the storage is back once | The cancel leaves a packet already queued |
| Raise against implicit cancel (the bound handle closed) | Exactly one packet, `signal` or `canceled` | The cancel does not look again, under the lock, at whether the binding is still armed: two packets from one reservation |
| Port close against raise | Nothing is queued once the close has returned; the storage is back once | Delivery does not look at the closed mark under the port's lock |
| Two raisers | One packet | The change from armed to fired is made outside the lock |
| Bind against close of the bound handle | Either the bind is refused and nothing is bound, or it is bound and a `canceled` packet follows; never an armed binding whose handle is gone | `still_open` is asked before the lock and not under it |
| Port close against bind | The bind is refused, or the close removes it; no binding stays armed on a closed port | The bind does not look at the closed mark |
| A taker against a delivery | A waiting taker gets the packet; no wake-up is lost | The waiter is woken before the packet is queued |
| Port close against a waiter | The waiter returns `Canceled` | The close wakes nobody |

**The proof programs.** Nothing new is needed from `proof_programs.rs`. Every line of the boot proof can be written with the script interpreter as it is: a call with up to six arguments, each an immediate or a `u32` handle loaded from a slot; a check of the status; `report_bytes` for the packet's 32 bytes and for `clock_read`'s 64-bit value. A deadline ahead is an immediate the kernel computes when it builds the script. The two-thread pattern the `wait_one` proof uses for `canceled` serves `port_wait`'s. No new assembly program.

```
    errors(fault, limit, no_memory) requires none;
@since(0.6) method object_wait_async = 16 (port: handle<port, write>, object: handle<any, wait>,
                                           key: u64, @nonzero signals: signals,)
    errors(bad_handle, wrong_type, invalid_args, access_denied, limit, no_memory) requires none;
@since(0.6) method port_wait = 17 (port: handle<port, read>, deadline: u64,) -> (packet: port_packet,)
    errors(bad_handle, wrong_type, access_denied, fault, timed_out, canceled) requires none;
@since(0.6) method port_cancel = 18 (port: handle<port, write>, object: handle<any>, key: u64,)
    errors(bad_handle, wrong_type, access_denied, not_found) requires none;
@since(0.6) method clock_read = 19 () -> (nanos: u64,)
    errors(fault) requires none;
```

`event_create` and `object_signal` are in the definition today as `@reserved` methods with `errors(not_supported)` (OIDL §4.4; its question Q11 kept them). They lose `@reserved` and take the error sets above. That changes a method's errors, which a major version forbids and major 0 reports without refusing (OIDL §9); `not_supported` leaves both sets, since a method that decodes no longer answers it.

1. **`port_packet` is a plain, frozen 32-byte struct of C9's six fields in C9's order; `packet_kind` is `signal = 1`, `canceled = 2`; `reserved` and `data` are zero in v1.** The 32 bytes are the scope document's figure and follow from the widths: a `u64` key, three `u32`s, a `u32` `reserved`, a `u64` `data`, no padding. 0 is never a kind, so a zeroed buffer is not a packet. **This is an exception to P4**, which says every structure crossing the boundary begins with a `u32 size`; The definition has plain records today, `received_handle` and `start_entry`, but each is an element inside a vector or a larger record. The only record a call writes by itself is `read_sizes`, and it is sized; `start_header` carries a `size` field of its own and is no precedent either way. So `port_packet` would be the first plain record a call writes alone through an out pointer. OIDL §3.1 already allows that (its row for a plain struct: a pointer as a parameter, an out pointer as a result), so OIDL needs no revision; stage 2 confirms early that the generator handles a plain struct as a lone result. A sized result makes the caller set `size` before every call and the kernel read and check it on the path that should be shortest. The room to grow is `reserved`, `data` and new kinds; a new layout would be a new call. Rejected: a sized struct with `size` where `reserved` is (also 32 bytes, but C9 lists `reserved` and not `size`); a larger packet sized for asynchronous I/O completions, a design not made yet; a timestamp or a count in `data` (a clock reading on every delivery, and a meaning later kinds could not take back).
2. **`object_wait_async(port, object, key, signals)` needs `write` on the port and `wait` on the object, takes no options argument, and its `signals` is non-zero with declared bits only.** A must-be-zero options word is what D1 decided against for creation calls, and the one option the model has, edge-triggering, C9 excludes; from major 1 an option is a new call. The first failure wins in D5's order: decoding (`invalid_args` for `signals` of 0 or an undeclared bit, as A2 has for `wait_one`), the port's handle, then the object's (`bad_handle`, `wrong_type`, `access_denied`), what is asked (an object that raises no signals, reachable only through a handle the kernel made, is `bad_handle` as `wait_one` answers), the quota (`limit`), the storage (`no_memory`, the charge returned). If the signal is already raised the packet is queued before the call returns (C9: level-triggered). If another thread closes the bound handle or the port during the call, the answer is `bad_handle` and nothing stays bound. `key` is opaque: compared, never interpreted. Rejected: an options word now; `wait` on the port (C6 gives a port none).
3. **`port_wait(port, deadline) -> packet` needs `read`; the deadline is `wait_one`'s (absolute, monotonic, nanoseconds; 0 polls; `deadline_infinite` never expires); `timed_out` when nothing is queued by then; `canceled` when the handle waited through is closed or moved during the wait.** `canceled` follows the handle's value, as `wait_one` does under A2, even if a duplicate keeps the port alive; no packet is consumed by a wait that ends that way. The out pointer is checked before the wait and the buffer is written only on `ok`. A packet is consumed when it is taken; if the copy then faults because the program unmapped its buffer during the wait, the answer is `fault` and the packet is gone, as `channel_read` treats a message it has taken. Putting it back could make a packet appear after a `port_cancel` that had already returned. The status `canceled` (this wait was cancelled) is not the packet kind `canceled` (a binding was). Rejected: returning a packet together with `timed_out` or `canceled`; `bad_handle` for a port closed during the wait (A2 says `canceled`; Windows fails the wait too).
4. **`port_cancel(port, object, key)` needs `write` on the port and no right on the object; it matches the port, the object behind the handle given, and the key; it removes every armed binding and every queued packet of either kind that matches, silently; `not_found` when nothing matched.** C9 says "for that object and key", so any handle to the object names it. A program that has closed its only handle to an object can no longer name it here; the `canceled` packet that close made is read like any other. The authority is the port's `write`: processes that share a port's `write` share control of its bindings, and the threat model gets a row for it. `not_found` is in the definition since 0.4: C11 added it for the service directory, the only case its doc comment names. This is its second use; its meaning ("nothing by that name or key is here") does not change, and its doc comment gains the port's case in stage 2. Rejected: always `ok` (a caller could not tell a late cancel from a wrong key); requiring `wait` on the object; letting only the binder cancel (illumos's rule; a server could not then clear a port it was handed).
5. **`object_signal` keeps its reserved signature; a non-event is `wrong_type`; a declared bit other than `signaled` in either mask is `invalid_args`; two empty masks are `ok` and change nothing; clear then set is one atomic update, `(old & !clear) | set`.** A bit in both masks ends set. There is no pulse: a signal raised and cleared before anything looked may go unseen, which is what level-triggered means. Order: undeclared bits (`invalid_args`, in decoding), the handle (`bad_handle`, then `access_denied` — C6 gives `signal` to events only, so an ordinary program meets this before `wrong_type`), the type (`wrong_type`), then the bits. Rejected: narrowing the parameter to `handle<event, signal>` (C10 says "in v1", and a later widening would change the signature); `not_supported` for a non-event (that status means the call is absent).
6. **`event_rights = wait | signal | duplicate | transfer` and `port_rights = read | write | duplicate | transfer`.** C6 word for word; only the names are new, after `channel_rights`, `thread_rights` and `memory_object_rights`.
7. **Three new quotas, each a count: `events`, `ports`, `bindings`. "Charged to the binder" is the `bindings` quota of the process that called `object_wait_async`, held from the bind until that binding's one packet has left the port.** Events and ports are charged to their creator until the object is destroyed, as channels are. Past a quota is `limit`, no memory is `no_memory`, and a process given no quota gets nothing (D4). A count and not bytes, because a binding has one size and a count keeps its meaning if the framework's node changes. There is no kernel-wide bound yet: today only the kernel's proofs create processes, and the Loader Service's defaults are step 4's. Rejected: charging bindings to `message_bytes` (a different resource, and a server's message budget would shrink with its bindings); one count for every kind of object.
8. **`wait_one` on an event works; on a port it is `access_denied`.** C6 gives a port no `wait` and no operation adds a right. A port raises no signals in v1, so through a kernel-made handle it answers `bad_handle` like any object that raises none, and a port cannot be bound to a port. Rejected: a `readable` signal on ports (it would let ports nest, and with that a cycle of ports).
9. **The same port, object and key may be bound twice: two bindings, two packets, each with its own storage and charge.** `port_cancel` removes every match. Rejected: refusing (a search on every bind, and a status the interface does not have); replacing (kqueue's and illumos's rule: the old binding may be firing as it is replaced).
10. **`clock_read() -> nanos` reads the monotonic clock only, takes no clock argument, and its one status is `fault`.** A call with a result cannot have no status: the result goes through an out pointer (P4, OIDL §10). A wall clock is a different call with different rules (§68). What the clock does across suspend is not decided here. Rejected: a clock-id argument (D1's reasoning: an argument with one legal value); the time in RAX (P4 makes RAX the status).
11. **A port with bindings.** *Transferred:* the binder stays charged; the charge follows the binding, and the port's own charge stays with its creator. *Its last handle closed:* every binding and queued packet goes, silently, and every charge is refunded. *The binder exits while another process holds the port:* exit closes every handle (P11), so each armed binding becomes a `canceled` packet (C9); those, and packets already queued, stay queued and stay charged to the exited binder's account until they are read or cancelled or the port closes — as a queued message stays charged to a sender that has exited. **The task's contract asks that a binder's exit leave no charge and no binding. Under this shape it leaves no armed binding, and no charge once the port is read, cancelled or closed; it leaves no charge at once only when the port dies with the binder, which is the usual case.** Rejected: removing an exited binder's bindings and packets silently (a reader on a shared port would wait for a packet that never comes, and C9 says an implicit cancel delivers); moving the charge to whoever holds the port (a charge nobody agreed to).
12. **A binding's storage is refunded when its packet is read, when it is explicitly cancelled, and when the port closes.** An implicit cancel refunds nothing: the storage becomes the `canceled` packet.
13. **`signals` may name any declared bits; a bit the object's type never raises never fires.** As `wait_one`. Rejected: checking the mask against the type (a table of which type raises what, which would become part of the interface).
14. **`trigger` is the mask the binding asked. `observed` is all of the object's signals at the moment the packet was queued — for a `canceled` packet, at the cancel.** A queued packet stays if the signal later falls: the binding was one-shot and has fired. Rejected: only the bits that fired (`trigger & observed` gives them); the signals at the time of the read (a queued packet would have to keep its object alive; see the framework section).
15. **Packets leave a port in the order they were queued, and a port has no bound of its own.** Every packet is some binding's reserved storage, so the binders' quotas bound the queue. Which waiting thread receives a packet is unspecified. Rejected: a per-port limit (a second bound on the same storage, with a new failure at delivery, where nothing may fail).
16. **An implicit cancel follows the handle the binding was made through, not the object.** A binding remembers its binder and that handle's value. Closing or moving that handle cancels, even if a duplicate still reaches the object; closing another duplicate does nothing; `handle_duplicate` copies no binding, and a moved handle's binding does not travel with it. A transfer cancels only if the `channel_write` succeeds: a refused write changes nothing. The port side goes by the object: closing the port handle used at bind while another keeps the port alive leaves the binding. A `signal` packet already queued when its handle closes stays, and no `canceled` packet follows it: one binding, one packet. Rejected: cancelling when the object's last handle goes (epoll's rule, and the surprise its page warns of; C9 says "the bound handle").
17. **`event_create()` and `port_create()` take no argument (D1) and answer `fault`, `limit` or `no_memory`; an event starts with no signal raised.** The out pointer is checked before anything is made, as `channel_create` does.
18. **Any object `wait_one` can wait on can be bound:** channel ends, processes, threads and events. Rejected: events only in v1 (one thread could not then serve many channels, which is what P9 gives ports for).
19. **Numbers: 9 and 10 as reserved; 15 `port_create`, 16 `object_wait_async`, 17 `port_wait`, 18 `port_cancel`, 19 `clock_read`.** Private to the release (P4); recorded, not a decision.
20. **Not in this task: packets a program queues itself, edge-triggered or repeating bindings, and the `Timer` object.** The first two are each a new call later. The `Timer` object is different: ADR-0005 P3 lists it among the kernel's objects, and the step-3 scope lists it as missing and gives it no task. It stays owed under P3 (the coordinating session records it as TASKS item 36, with these decisions). Until it exists, a deadline on `port_wait` or `wait_one`, with `clock_read`, covers a timeout.

## Tests defined before the code (§16 step 12)

- **Host, at least one per rule of C9:** a binding fires once, and a second signal makes no second packet; a signal already raised at bind queues the packet before the call returns; the key comes back unchanged, including 0 and `u64::MAX`; the storage is reserved and the quota charged at bind, and a bind past the quota or without memory changes nothing; closing the bound handle queues a `canceled` packet; moving it queues one, and a refused move queues none; `port_cancel` queues nothing; `port_cancel` removes a packet already queued, of either kind; the same key bound again right after a cancel fires normally; the packet's six fields and its 32 bytes, field by field against the generated layout; one `port_wait` returns one packet, in queue order. **C10:** `object_signal` on a non-event and on a bit other than `signaled`; clear-then-set, a bit in both masks, two empty masks. **Shapes:** each of 2 to 5 and 8 to 16 has a test named for it; quotas are checked to the unit (charged at bind, refunded on read, on cancel and on close, not on an implicit cancel) and every account ends at zero.
- **Loom**, on the shipping protocol, each model with at least one committed control in xtask's `CONTROLS`: the nine models in the framework section.

## Unknown

- **Nothing here has run.** This stage is documents; every figure and every claim of behaviour above is a design until stage 2.
- **Whether 12 spare bytes are enough for an I/O completion.** Shape 1 leaves `reserved` and `data` for later kinds; asynchronous I/O is not designed, so nothing shows they suffice.
- **What a binder's exit should leave on a shared port** (shape 11): the recommendation keeps C9's packet and bends the contract's wording. It is the owner's call.
- **Fairness among waiting threads, and more than 16 of them.** Past 16 they poll by yielding; no order is promised.
- **A deadline's resolution** is the tick, 10 ms today (scope §5); `port_wait` inherits it.
- **A thread blocked in `port_wait` when another process terminates its process.** Killing a blocked thread needs a framework call, and termination by another process is out of this step's scope (scope §5); `port_wait` blocks as `wait_one` does and adds nothing to that.
- **CR4.TSD.** Left clear. Setting it, and what `clock_read`'s resolution would then be, is ADR-0017's and the owner's.
- **The clock across suspend, a wall clock, a shared clock page** (C13, ADR-0012 C7, §68): later calls and decisions.
- **Kernel-wide bounds and the defaults a process is given** (step 4): until then a quota is whatever the creator passes, and only the kernel's proofs create processes.
- **`object_signal` on other objects:** not in v1 (C10).
- **Packets a program queues itself, edge-triggered or repeating bindings:** not in this task; each a new call later.
- **The `Timer` object:** not in this task. ADR-0005 P3 lists it, so it stays owed (TASKS item 36, to be recorded with the decisions). A deadline and `clock_read` cover a timeout until then.
- **E1 in the proof interpreter:** it loads a `u32` handle today; 64-bit handles will need a wider load. E1's, not this task's.
- **Not read:** the seL4 reference manual on notifications; AMD's APM Volume 2 on CR4.TSD (Volume 3's instruction pages state the rule). Fuchsia's port concept page is a stub, and its reference pages do not say when a packet's memory is reserved or what a wait returns when the port's handle closes.
- **No hardware.** When this is built it runs under QEMU (TCG) with OVMF on an M1 Max; nothing will have run on an x86-64 processor.
